RDSS Scheme

Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme – Transforming India's Electricity Distribution System

1. What is the RDSS Scheme?

The Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme (RDSS) is a Government of India initiative launched to improve the operational efficiency and financial sustainability of electricity distribution companies (DISCOMs).

The scheme focuses on modernising electricity distribution infrastructure, reducing power losses, installing smart meters and improving the quality and reliability of electricity supply to consumers.

It follows a reforms-based and results-linked approach, meaning that financial assistance is connected to performance, reforms and the achievement of prescribed conditions.

Full Form: Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme (RDSS).

2. Launch Date and Ministry

  • Launch date: 20 July 2021.
  • Ministry: Ministry of Power, Government of India.
  • Nodal agencies: Rural Electrification Corporation (REC) and Power Finance Corporation (PFC).
  • Scheme type: Central Sector reforms-based and results-linked scheme.
  • Original duration: FY 2021–22 to FY 2025–26.
  • Extended sunset date: March 2028.

3. Main Objectives of RDSS

The main objective is to make electricity distribution companies financially stronger and improve the quality of electricity supplied to consumers.

  • Reduce Aggregate Technical and Commercial (AT&C) losses.
  • Eliminate the gap between the Average Cost of Supply (ACS) and Average Revenue Realised (ARR).
  • Improve the quality and reliability of electricity supply.
  • Modernise electricity distribution infrastructure.
  • Promote prepaid smart metering.
  • Improve billing and collection efficiency.
  • Reduce power theft and unauthorised electricity consumption.
  • Improve the financial health of DISCOMs.
  • Encourage digital monitoring of electricity distribution networks.

4. Total Budget and Financial Outlay

The Government of India approved a total outlay of ₹3,03,758 crore for the RDSS programme.

  • Total scheme outlay: ₹3,03,758 crore.
  • Estimated Gross Budgetary Support: ₹97,631 crore.
  • Original scheme period: FY 2021–22 to FY 2025–26.
  • Extended sunset date: March 2028.
The scheme's financial support is primarily directed towards eligible electricity distribution utilities and approved infrastructure projects. It is not a direct cash subsidy for ordinary electricity consumers.

5. Major Components of RDSS

RDSS has two major components: Part A and Part B.

  • Part A – Financial Support: Supports prepaid smart metering, system metering, distribution infrastructure upgrades and loss-reduction projects.
  • Part B – Enabling and Supporting Activities: Covers training, capacity building and other activities that help improve the functioning of electricity distribution utilities.
Remember: Part A focuses mainly on infrastructure and metering, while Part B focuses on capacity building and supporting reforms.

6. What is Smart Metering?

Smart meters are advanced electricity meters that digitally record electricity consumption and can communicate consumption information to the electricity distribution company.

Unlike conventional meters that generally require manual readings, smart meters can support remote monitoring and more frequent communication of electricity consumption data.

  • Digital recording of electricity consumption.
  • Remote meter reading where communication infrastructure is available.
  • Better monitoring of electricity usage.
  • Improved billing accuracy and transparency.
  • Support for prepaid electricity services.
  • Faster identification of certain metering and supply issues.

7. What is a Prepaid Smart Meter?

A prepaid smart meter works on a system similar to prepaid mobile recharge. Consumers can add money to their electricity account and use electricity according to their available balance.

  • Consumers can monitor their electricity consumption.
  • They can track their available prepaid balance.
  • They can recharge their account through supported payment channels.
  • Electricity usage information can be communicated digitally.
  • Consumers can receive alerts about low balances, where supported.
Prepaid smart meters do not mean free electricity. Consumers still pay for the electricity they use according to the applicable tariff and billing rules.

8. What is System Metering?

System metering means installing meters at different levels of the electricity distribution network. These meters help distribution companies monitor electricity entering and moving through the system.

  • Feeder meters: Measure electricity supplied through distribution feeders.
  • Distribution Transformer meters: Monitor electricity supplied through distribution transformers.
  • Consumer meters: Record electricity consumed by individual consumers.

Comparing the electricity supplied at different levels helps utilities identify technical and commercial losses.

9. What is AT&C Loss?

Aggregate Technical and Commercial (AT&C) loss is an important performance indicator used to measure the overall losses in an electricity distribution system.

It includes technical losses and commercial losses arising from issues such as inefficient infrastructure, electricity theft, inaccurate metering and uncollected electricity bills.

  • Technical losses: Electricity lost during transmission and distribution due to resistance and other physical factors.
  • Commercial losses: Losses associated with billing, metering, theft and collection problems.
RDSS originally aimed to reduce national AT&C losses to approximately 12–15%.

10. What is the ACS-ARR Gap?

ACS-ARR gap refers to the difference between the Average Cost of Supply (ACS) and Average Revenue Realised (ARR) by an electricity distribution utility.

  • ACS: Average cost incurred by a DISCOM to supply one unit of electricity.
  • ARR: Average revenue realised by the DISCOM for each unit of electricity sold.
  • ACS-ARR gap: Difference between the average cost and average revenue.

A large gap can create financial difficulties for electricity distribution companies.

RDSS aims to bring the ACS-ARR gap to zero through operational and financial reforms.

11. Infrastructure Development Under RDSS

The scheme supports modernisation and strengthening of electricity distribution networks.

  • Replacement of old and inefficient electricity conductors.
  • Construction of new substations.
  • Upgradation of existing substations.
  • Installation of additional distribution transformers.
  • Replacement and augmentation of overloaded transformers.
  • Feeder segregation where required.
  • Improvement of high-tension and low-tension distribution lines.
  • Installation of SCADA and Distribution Management Systems.

12. What is SCADA and DMS?

RDSS supports digital technologies that help electricity utilities monitor and manage distribution networks.

  • SCADA: Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition is a system used to monitor and control electrical equipment and network operations.
  • DMS: Distribution Management System helps utilities monitor, analyse and manage electricity distribution networks.

These technologies can help identify faults, monitor network conditions and improve operational decisions.

13. Who Can Receive Financial Assistance?

RDSS primarily provides financial support to eligible electricity distribution utilities rather than individual consumers.

  • State-owned electricity distribution companies (DISCOMs).
  • State and Union Territory Power Departments.
  • Eligible public-sector distribution utilities meeting the scheme's conditions.

Private-sector electricity distribution companies are excluded from the standard financial assistance coverage described in the scheme guidelines.

Individual households cannot directly apply for RDSS infrastructure grants. Project proposals are submitted by eligible electricity distribution utilities and state agencies.

14. Eligibility Conditions for DISCOMs

Financial assistance under RDSS is linked to reforms and performance requirements.

  • Submission of an approved Action Plan and Detailed Project Report (DPR).
  • Compliance with prescribed pre-qualifying criteria.
  • Improvement in operational and financial performance.
  • Achievement of required results under the evaluation framework.
  • Compliance with applicable accounting and regulatory requirements.
  • Timely payment of government subsidies and dues to utilities.
  • Implementation and monitoring of approved projects.
Release of assistance is linked to the scheme's pre-qualification requirements and results-evaluation framework.

15. Role of REC and PFC

Rural Electrification Corporation (REC) and Power Finance Corporation (PFC) are the nodal agencies responsible for facilitating implementation of RDSS.

  • Assist in processing project proposals.
  • Coordinate with electricity distribution utilities.
  • Monitor the progress of approved projects.
  • Review implementation and performance information.
  • Monitor smart metering and infrastructure works.
  • Support the release of financial assistance according to scheme guidelines.

16. How is RDSS Implemented?

The scheme follows a structured project approval and monitoring process.

  • State governments and DISCOMs identify infrastructure and reform requirements.
  • Eligible utilities prepare Action Plans and DPRs.
  • Project proposals are submitted for examination and approval.
  • Approved projects receive financial assistance according to applicable conditions.
  • Utilities implement infrastructure and smart metering works.
  • Progress is monitored by the nodal agencies and government committees.
  • Further fund releases are linked to prescribed reforms and performance requirements.

17. Latest Progress Under RDSS

According to the Ministry of Power's update dated 30 March 2026, substantial distribution infrastructure and smart metering projects had been sanctioned.

  • Total sanctioned project value: Approximately ₹2.83 lakh crore.
  • Loss-reduction infrastructure projects: Approximately ₹1.53 lakh crore.
  • Smart metering projects: Approximately ₹1.31 lakh crore.
  • AT&C losses: Reduced from 21.91% in FY 2020–21 to 15.04% in FY 2024–25 at the national level.
  • Billing efficiency: Improved from 84.08% to 87.59% over the same period.
  • Collection efficiency: Improved from 92.9% to 97% over the same period.
These are government-reported figures. Sanctioned project value should not be confused with the amount already spent or projects fully completed.

18. Smart Meter Installation Progress

Smart metering is one of the major components of RDSS.

According to a Ministry of Power update dated 2 February 2026, the installation position as of 31 December 2025 was:

  • Consumer smart meters sanctioned: 19.79 crore.
  • Distribution Transformer meters sanctioned: 52.53 lakh.
  • Feeder meters sanctioned: 2.05 lakh.
  • Consumer smart meters installed under RDSS: 3.90 crore.
  • Total smart meters installed across India under various schemes: 5.28 crore.
The 5.28 crore figure includes installations under other schemes and state plans. It is not the RDSS-only installation figure.

19. Consumer Benefits Under RDSS

Although RDSS mainly supports electricity distribution companies, its improvements are intended to benefit electricity consumers.

  • More reliable electricity supply.
  • Better monitoring of electricity consumption.
  • Improved billing accuracy.
  • Faster identification of electricity network problems.
  • Better management of distribution infrastructure.
  • Potential reduction in distribution losses.
  • Improved financial health of DISCOMs.

20. Consumer Safeguards and Smart Metering

The Ministry of Power has highlighted several consumer-related measures associated with smart meter implementation.

  • Rebates in electricity bills to incentivise prepaid meter installation, as applicable.
  • No penalty on consumers based on maximum demand recorded by smart meters, under the announced measure.
  • Mechanisms for recovery of past arrears through easier instalments.
  • Installation of check meters to improve consumer confidence in meter accuracy.
Actual tariff, rebate, billing and recovery arrangements depend on the applicable State Electricity Regulatory Commission orders and the consumer's DISCOM.

21. Difference Between RDSS and DDUGJY

  • DDUGJY: Focused on rural electrification, feeder separation and rural distribution infrastructure.
  • RDSS: Focuses on improving the overall operational and financial performance of electricity distribution utilities.
  • DDUGJY launch: December 2014.
  • RDSS launch: July 2021.
  • DDUGJY status: Closed on 31 March 2022.
  • RDSS status: Extended until March 2028.

22. Current Status of RDSS

RDSS continues as a major electricity distribution reform programme. Its sunset date has been extended to March 2028, according to the Central Electricity Authority's Annual Report 2025–26.

  • Original launch: July 2021.
  • Original scheme period: FY 2021–22 to FY 2025–26.
  • Revised sunset date: March 2028.
  • Implementing utilities: Eligible public-sector DISCOMs and State/UT Power Departments.
  • Individual applications: No direct household RDSS application process for infrastructure grants.
Consumers seeking a new electricity connection, reporting billing problems or requesting a meter replacement should contact their local DISCOM. RDSS is not an individual household subsidy portal.

25. Conclusion

The Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme is an important initiative for modernising India's electricity distribution system. Through smart metering, infrastructure upgrades and performance-linked financial assistance, it aims to improve the financial health of DISCOMs and the quality of electricity supply.

Its success is important for building a reliable, efficient and financially sustainable power distribution sector that can support India's growing electricity needs.
