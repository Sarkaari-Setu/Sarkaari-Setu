SATAT Scheme

Sustainable Alternative Towards Affordable Transportation

1. What is the SATAT Initiative?

SATAT stands for Sustainable Alternative Towards Affordable Transportation. It is an initiative launched by the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas (MoPNG), Government of India, on 1 October 2018.

Its main objective is to establish an ecosystem for producing Compressed Bio-Gas (CBG) from organic waste and biomass and promoting its use as a clean alternative to conventional fossil fuels.

Under SATAT, Oil and Gas Marketing Companies invite Expressions of Interest (EOIs) from entrepreneurs interested in setting up CBG plants. The companies can procure the produced CBG under agreed commercial arrangements and market it further.

Full Form: Sustainable Alternative Towards Affordable Transportation.
Launch Date: 1 October 2018.
Nodal Ministry: Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas.

2. Objectives of SATAT

  • Clean Fuel: Promote compressed biogas as an alternative to conventional transport and industrial fuels.
  • Waste Management: Encourage the conversion of agricultural, municipal and industrial organic waste into useful energy.
  • Energy Security: Reduce dependence on imported fossil fuels by increasing domestic renewable fuel production.
  • Farmer Income: Create additional economic opportunities through the productive use of agricultural residues and animal waste.
  • Rural Employment: Generate employment in feedstock collection, transportation, plant construction and operation.
  • Environmental Protection: Encourage better waste management and reduce unmanaged organic waste.
  • Circular Economy: Promote the use of waste-derived energy and organic manure in a resource-efficient economy.

3. What is Compressed Bio-Gas (CBG)?

Compressed Bio-Gas is a purified and compressed form of biogas produced from biodegradable organic materials through anaerobic digestion.

Raw biogas generally contains methane, carbon dioxide and small quantities of other gases. During upgrading, unwanted gases and impurities are removed to increase methane concentration.

The purified gas is then compressed to make it suitable for storage, transportation and use as fuel.

  • CBG has properties similar to compressed natural gas (CNG) when it meets the applicable fuel-quality standards.
  • It can be supplied to suitable gas distribution networks.
  • It can be used in compatible vehicles and other approved applications.
  • It is produced using renewable organic resources.
CBG and ordinary biogas are not exactly the same. CBG undergoes purification and compression before being supplied as a transport or commercial fuel.

4. Raw Materials Used for CBG Production

SATAT promotes CBG production from different waste and biomass sources.

A. Agricultural Waste

  • Paddy straw and crop residues.
  • Sugarcane residues.
  • Agricultural processing waste.
  • Other suitable biodegradable biomass.

B. Animal Waste

  • Cattle dung.
  • Livestock waste.
  • Poultry waste.
  • Other suitable animal-based organic materials.

C. Municipal Organic Waste

  • Food waste.
  • Vegetable and fruit market waste.
  • Segregated biodegradable municipal waste.
  • Other suitable organic waste streams.

D. Industrial Waste

  • Food-processing waste.
  • Dairy industry waste.
  • Suitable organic industrial effluents.
  • Other eligible biodegradable industrial residues.
The feedstock must be suitable for the selected plant technology. Availability, quality, moisture content and contamination are important considerations when planning a CBG project.

5. How Does the SATAT System Work?

SATAT connects CBG producers with Oil and Gas Marketing Companies (OGMCs), creating a commercial route for selling the gas.

  • Step 1 – Feedstock Collection: Entrepreneurs arrange organic waste and biomass from suitable sources.
  • Step 2 – Biogas Production: Organic materials are processed through anaerobic digestion or another suitable technology.
  • Step 3 – Gas Purification: Raw biogas is upgraded to remove carbon dioxide and other unwanted components.
  • Step 4 – Compression: The purified gas is compressed to produce CBG.
  • Step 5 – Quality Testing: The gas is checked against the applicable quality and safety standards.
  • Step 6 – Supply: CBG is transported or delivered through an approved supply arrangement.
  • Step 7 – Marketing: The procuring company markets the gas for eligible applications.
The plant owner is generally responsible for establishing, operating and maintaining the CBG plant, while the Oil Marketing Company provides a potential procurement route under the applicable agreement.

6. Oil Marketing Companies Under SATAT

Several public-sector Oil and Gas Marketing Companies have participated in the SATAT initiative by inviting expressions of interest and facilitating CBG procurement.

  • Indian Oil Corporation Limited (IOCL)
  • Bharat Petroleum Corporation Limited (BPCL)
  • Hindustan Petroleum Corporation Limited (HPCL)
  • GAIL (India) Limited
  • Indraprastha Gas Limited (IGL)

These organisations have played roles in CBG procurement, marketing and development of the gas ecosystem.

The specific company, procurement location, quality specifications and commercial conditions depend on the relevant Expression of Interest and agreement.

7. Original SATAT Target

At the time of its launch, SATAT envisaged developing a large network of CBG production facilities across India.

  • 5,000 CBG plants: Original programme vision for establishing a nationwide production ecosystem.
  • 15 million metric tonnes per annum: Original envisaged CBG production target.
  • 50 million tonnes of manure: Original envisaged production of organic manure as a by-product.
These were original programme targets and should not be presented as the current achieved production figures or a guarantee of plant installation.

8. Who Can Participate in SATAT?

SATAT is primarily designed to encourage entrepreneurs and organisations to establish CBG production facilities.

Potential participants include:

  • Private entrepreneurs.
  • Companies and industrial organisations.
  • Farmers' organisations and cooperatives.
  • Eligible agricultural and dairy businesses.
  • Waste-management companies.
  • Other eligible project developers.

Applicants must have a commercially and technically feasible project, adequate feedstock arrangements and the ability to meet the applicable project requirements.

SATAT is not a general household subsidy programme. An individual does not automatically receive money simply for owning cattle, agricultural land or producing organic waste.

9. Does SATAT Provide a Government Subsidy?

One of the most important points to understand is that SATAT itself is not a direct capital-subsidy scheme for establishing CBG plants.

In a Lok Sabha reply dated 6 August 2026, the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas clarified that the Government does not provide financial assistance for setting up CBG plants under the SATAT initiative.

However, other government programmes and policy measures can support eligible CBG projects.

  • GOBARdhan National Circular Bioenergy Scheme: Provides a separate framework for eligible capital assistance, offtake, pricing, pipeline infrastructure and credit support.
  • MNRE Waste-to-Energy Programme: Previously provided Central Financial Assistance for eligible projects under its applicable guidelines.
  • Development of Pipeline Infrastructure: Provides support for eligible pipeline connectivity projects under separate guidelines.
  • Priority Sector Lending: RBI has included eligible CBG projects within the Priority Sector Lending framework.
Do not confuse procurement agreements under SATAT with government subsidy. Each separate financial-support programme has its own eligibility conditions and application process.

10. How Does CBG Procurement Work?

Under SATAT, Oil and Gas Marketing Companies invite Expressions of Interest from potential CBG producers.

  • Entrepreneurs identify a suitable project and assess its technical and commercial feasibility.
  • They respond to the relevant EOI issued by an Oil Marketing Company.
  • The proposed plant and its production capacity are assessed under the applicable process.
  • Eligible producers may enter into a commercial agreement for CBG supply.
  • The plant owner produces CBG according to the agreed specifications.
  • The procuring company receives the gas under the agreed delivery and commercial terms.

The original SATAT framework envisaged long-term offtake agreements, commonly described as 15-year commercial agreements, subject to the terms of the applicable agreement.

An EOI is an invitation to express interest. It is not an automatic guarantee of project approval, financing or a purchase agreement.

11. Important Policy Support for SATAT

The Government has introduced several policy measures to support the wider CBG ecosystem.

  • Priority Sector Lending: RBI included eligible CBG projects under Priority Sector Lending through its September 2020 directions.
  • Organic Manure: Bio-manure produced from CBG plants was included as Fermented Organic Manure under the Fertilizer Control Order, 1985, through a notification dated 13 July 2020.
  • Long-Term Offtake: Oil and Gas Marketing Companies have entered into long-term procurement arrangements with eligible producers.
  • CBG-CGD Synchronisation: A separate framework supports the integration of CBG production with City Gas Distribution networks.
  • Pipeline Infrastructure: Separate policy support is available for eligible pipeline connectivity projects.
  • Financing: Financial institutions and banks have developed financing options for eligible CBG projects.

12. CBG-CGD Synchronisation Scheme

The CBG-CGD Synchronisation Scheme is a separate policy framework designed to integrate CBG producers with City Gas Distribution (CGD) networks.

It helps connect CBG production with the existing natural gas distribution system, subject to technical and commercial requirements.

  • Provides a route for eligible CBG producers to supply gas to CGD networks.
  • Supports the blending of CBG with natural gas in eligible networks.
  • Promotes the use of existing gas distribution infrastructure.
  • Helps create a wider market for renewable gas.
CBG must meet the required gas-quality, pressure, metering and safety standards before it can be injected into an authorised gas network.

13. Application and Participation Process

Entrepreneurs interested in SATAT should first identify the relevant procurement opportunity and assess the feasibility of their proposed CBG plant.

  • Identify suitable organic waste and biomass sources.
  • Assess the availability and long-term reliability of feedstock.
  • Prepare a preliminary technical and financial project report.
  • Review the current EOI issued by the relevant Oil Marketing Company.
  • Check the eligibility, location and gas-quality requirements.
  • Submit the required expression of interest and project information.
  • Complete the commercial and technical assessment process.
  • Enter into an agreement if selected and approved.
  • Establish and operate the plant according to applicable approvals and standards.

14. Documents and Project Information

The exact requirements vary according to the Oil Marketing Company's EOI and the project category.

  • Applicant or company registration details.
  • Project location and land information.
  • Proposed plant capacity.
  • Details of technology and production process.
  • Feedstock availability and supply arrangements.
  • Estimated project cost and financial plan.
  • Details of proposed gas delivery and transportation.
  • Environmental and statutory approvals, where applicable.
  • Technical and financial feasibility reports.
This is a general preparation checklist, not a mandatory document list for every EOI. Always follow the actual application requirements.

15. Benefits of SATAT

  • Encourages production of renewable fuel from organic waste.
  • Creates a potential commercial market for CBG producers.
  • Promotes better utilisation of agricultural residues and cattle dung.
  • Supports additional income opportunities across the rural supply chain.
  • Encourages private-sector investment in renewable energy.
  • Supports the development of CBG transportation and distribution infrastructure.
  • Produces organic manure as a useful by-product.
  • Contributes to India's efforts to reduce dependence on imported fossil fuels.

16. Latest SATAT Status – November 2026

SATAT continues to form part of India's broader CBG development ecosystem. However, the Government has moved towards a more integrated framework for the sector through the GOBARdhan National Circular Bioenergy Scheme.

Important figures reported in 2026:

  • As of 31 July 2026, 217 CBG plants had been commissioned across India, with an aggregate production capacity of approximately 1,773 tonnes per day.
  • Of these, 180 plants were reported under SATAT and the CBG-CGD Synchronisation Scheme.
  • The remaining commissioned plants were associated with other project routes.
  • The figures refer to commissioned plants, not all plants registered, proposed or under construction.

On 6 August 2026, the Union Cabinet approved the GOBARdhan National Circular Bioenergy Scheme with a total outlay of ₹23,731 crore.

The scheme was officially launched on 1 October 2026 and is designed to provide an integrated framework for the CBG sector from FY 2026–27 to FY 2035–36.

SATAT should not be confused with the new GOBARdhan scheme. SATAT provides a procurement and market-development framework, while GOBARdhan introduces a broader package of support for eligible CBG projects.

17. GOBARdhan – The New CBG Support Framework

The GOBARdhan National Circular Bioenergy Scheme was approved in August 2026 and launched in October 2026.

Key features include:

  • Total Outlay: ₹23,731 crore.
  • Implementation Period: FY 2026–27 to FY 2035–36.
  • Capital Assistance: Eligible greenfield CBG projects can receive assistance of up to ₹2 crore per tonne per day (TPD) of installed capacity, subject to scheme conditions.
  • Administered Price: A CBG price framework of ₹2,110 per MMBtu has been introduced.
  • Offtake Assurance: Procurement obligations are assigned to eligible City Gas Distribution entities.
  • Pipeline Support: Financial assistance is available for eligible pipeline connectivity.
  • Credit Guarantee: A dedicated mechanism supports eligible MSME-based CBG projects.

The scheme aims to increase domestic CBG production nearly ten-fold, reaching around 5 MMSCMD.

Visit Unified GOBARdhan Portal
Project developers should check the new scheme's detailed guidelines and portal requirements before applying. The announced maximum support is not an automatic entitlement for every CBG plant.

18. Difference Between SATAT and GOBARdhan

SATAT Initiative

  • Launched on 1 October 2018.
  • Nodal Ministry: Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas.
  • Focuses on developing the CBG market through procurement by Oil and Gas Marketing Companies.
  • Invites EOIs from entrepreneurs interested in producing CBG.
  • Does not itself provide capital financial assistance for establishing CBG plants.

GOBARdhan National Circular Bioenergy Scheme

  • Approved on 6 August 2026 and launched on 1 October 2026.
  • Nodal Ministry: Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas.
  • Provides a wider framework for CBG production and infrastructure development.
  • Includes capital assistance, pricing support, offtake assurance and credit support.
  • Has a total outlay of ₹23,731 crore for FY 2026–27 to FY 2035–36.
Both initiatives contribute to the development of India's CBG ecosystem, but their financial provisions and implementation structures are different.
