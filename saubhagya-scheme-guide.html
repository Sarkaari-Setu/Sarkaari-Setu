Saubhagya Scheme

Pradhan Mantri Sahaj Bijli Har Ghar Yojana – Bringing Electricity to Every Willing Household

1. What is the Saubhagya Scheme?

Saubhagya, officially known as Pradhan Mantri Sahaj Bijli Har Ghar Yojana, was a Government of India initiative launched to provide electricity connections to willing unelectrified households across the country.

The programme focused on providing last-mile electricity connectivity to rural households and poor households in urban areas. It also provided standalone solar photovoltaic systems to certain remote households where extending the electricity grid was not feasible or cost-effective.

Full Form: Pradhan Mantri Sahaj Bijli Har Ghar Yojana (SAUBHAGYA).

2. Launch Date and Ministry

  • Launch date: 25 September 2017.
  • Implementation period: 2017–2022.
  • Ministry: Ministry of Power, Government of India.
  • Nodal agency: Rural Electrification Corporation (REC).
  • Scheme type: Central Government household electrification programme.
  • Main objective: Universal household electricity access.
The scheme was launched in September 2017 and its implementation was carried out from October 2017 onward.

3. Main Objectives of Saubhagya

The primary objective was to make electricity accessible to households that had not yet received an electricity connection.

  • Provide electricity connections to willing unelectrified rural households.
  • Provide connections to willing poor households in urban areas.
  • Improve access to electricity in remote and inaccessible areas.
  • Provide last-mile electricity connectivity.
  • Reduce dependence on kerosene for household lighting.
  • Improve educational opportunities through better lighting.
  • Support access to communication and information technology.
  • Improve the overall quality of life in rural and urban communities.

4. Who Was Eligible?

The scheme covered different categories of households depending on their location and economic status.

  • Rural households: All willing unelectrified households in rural areas.
  • Urban households: Willing poor unelectrified households in urban areas.
  • SECC beneficiaries: Eligible poor households identified using Socio-Economic and Caste Census (SECC) 2011 data, including households meeting the prescribed deprivation criteria.
  • Remote households: Unelectrified households in inaccessible locations where grid extension was not feasible could receive standalone solar systems.
Non-poor urban households were not included in the free-connection beneficiary category under Saubhagya.

5. What Benefits Were Provided?

The programme supported the release of electricity connections and the required basic infrastructure for household electrification.

  • Electricity service cable from the nearest electricity pole to the household.
  • Installation of an electricity energy meter.
  • Basic internal wiring for a single light point.
  • Provision of an LED bulb.
  • A mobile charging point.
  • Associated electrical accessories as per the scheme specifications.
Saubhagya primarily provided an electricity connection. It did not mean unlimited free electricity consumption or permanently free monthly electricity bills.

6. Free Electricity Connection and Charges

Eligible beneficiaries could receive electricity connections without paying the connection cost under the scheme.

  • Eligible rural households could receive free electricity connections.
  • Eligible poor urban households could also receive free connections.
  • Households not identified as eligible through the prescribed SECC criteria could obtain connections by paying ₹500 under the original scheme provisions.
  • The ₹500 amount was recoverable by the DISCOM or Power Department in 10 instalments through electricity bills.
These were historical scheme provisions. Saubhagya is now closed, so the old payment arrangement should not be treated as a current application offer.

7. Special Provision for Remote Areas

Some households were located in remote and inaccessible areas where extending the electricity distribution grid was not feasible or cost-effective.

For such households, the scheme provided for standalone Solar Photovoltaic (SPV) systems.

  • Solar power pack capacity of approximately 200–300 Wp.
  • Battery storage backup.
  • Up to five LED lights.
  • One DC fan.
  • One DC power plug.
  • Repair and maintenance support for five years.
This provision helped remote households access basic electricity without depending on conventional grid extension.

8. Total Budget and Financial Outlay

The original approved financial outlay of Saubhagya was ₹16,320 crore.

  • Total estimated outlay: ₹16,320 crore.
  • Gross Budgetary Support: ₹12,320 crore.
  • Implementing agency: Electricity distribution utilities and other eligible implementing entities.
  • Funding: Central Government grants, state or utility contribution and institutional loans as prescribed in the scheme.
The financial assistance under Saubhagya was primarily provided for electricity infrastructure and connection implementation through eligible agencies.

9. Role of REC and DISCOMs

The programme involved coordination between the Central Government, state governments and electricity distribution companies.

  • REC: Served as the nodal agency for the scheme.
  • DISCOMs: Identified households and implemented electricity connections.
  • State Power Departments: Supported implementation and monitoring.
  • Local authorities: Helped identify eligible households and coordinate local activities.

10. How Was the Scheme Implemented?

Electricity distribution companies and state agencies carried out household identification and connection activities.

  • Identification of unelectrified households.
  • Verification of beneficiary eligibility.
  • Survey of electricity infrastructure requirements.
  • Installation of service cables and meters.
  • Provision of basic wiring and lighting accessories.
  • Installation of standalone solar systems in eligible remote locations.
  • Monitoring of household electrification progress.

11. How Could Beneficiaries Apply?

During the implementation period, electricity distribution companies organised village-level and cluster-level camps to identify eligible households and facilitate applications.

  • Households could approach their local electricity distribution company.
  • Beneficiaries could participate in local electrification camps.
  • DISCOM officials collected household and identification details.
  • Eligible households were verified before the electricity connection was released.
Current status: Saubhagya is closed as of 31 March 2022. New applicants cannot apply for a fresh connection through this closed scheme.

12. Documents Required During Implementation

The implementing electricity utility collected the information needed to identify and verify households. Depending on the circumstances, this could include:

  • Household name and address.
  • Identity proof, where available.
  • SECC-related beneficiary information, where applicable.
  • Mobile number, where available.
  • Other identification details accepted by the DISCOM.
This is a historical indicative list, not a current Saubhagya application checklist.

13. Achievements of Saubhagya

Saubhagya played a major role in India's household electrification efforts.

  • Approximately 2.86 crore households were electrified during the Saubhagya implementation period.
  • States reported 100% electrification of willing unelectrified households identified before 31 March 2019.
  • Remote households received standalone solar systems where grid extension was not feasible.
  • The programme improved access to electricity for households across rural and urban areas.
The Ministry of Power reported these achievements in government statements, including a February 2026 parliamentary update.

14. Current Status of the Scheme

All sanctioned works under Saubhagya have been completed. The Government of India has confirmed that the scheme stands closed from 31 March 2022.

  • Scheme status: Closed.
  • Closure date: 31 March 2022.
  • Households electrified: Approximately 2.86 crore.
  • New Saubhagya applications: Not currently open.

However, the Government continues to support the electrification of identified left-out households through the Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme (RDSS), based on surveys conducted by electricity distribution utilities and applicable programme guidelines.

Do not confuse RDSS with Saubhagya. RDSS is a separate programme with its own eligibility and implementation arrangements.

15. Difference Between Saubhagya and RDSS

  • Saubhagya: Focused on providing electricity connections to willing unelectrified rural households and poor urban households.
  • RDSS: Focuses on improving the efficiency and financial sustainability of electricity distribution systems, along with supporting identified household electrification works.
  • Saubhagya status: Closed on 31 March 2022.
  • RDSS status: A separate ongoing government programme.

16. Benefits of Household Electrification

  • Education: Better lighting supports students in studying after sunset.
  • Healthcare: Electricity can improve the functioning of health facilities and essential equipment.
  • Communication: Households can charge mobile phones and use electronic devices.
  • Safety: Electric lighting can improve visibility inside and around homes.
  • Economic development: Electricity can support small businesses and productive activities.
  • Quality of life: Households gain access to modern electrical services.

17. Important Points to Remember

  • Saubhagya stands for Pradhan Mantri Sahaj Bijli Har Ghar Yojana.
  • It was launched on 25 September 2017.
  • It was implemented by the Ministry of Power.
  • REC was the nodal agency.
  • Its main aim was universal household electrification.
  • It covered willing unelectrified rural households and poor urban households.
  • Remote households could receive standalone solar systems.
  • Approximately 2.86 crore households were electrified during the scheme period.
  • The scheme closed on 31 March 2022.

19. Conclusion

The Saubhagya Scheme was an important initiative in India's journey towards universal household electrification. By providing last-mile electricity connections and standalone solar systems in remote locations, it helped millions of households gain access to electricity.

Although Saubhagya is now closed, household electrification efforts continue through other government programmes, including RDSS, for identified households that still require electricity connections.
