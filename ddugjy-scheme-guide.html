DDUGJY Scheme

Deen Dayal Upadhyaya Gram Jyoti Yojana – Strengthening Rural Electricity Infrastructure Across India

1. What is the DDUGJY Scheme?

Deen Dayal Upadhyaya Gram Jyoti Yojana (DDUGJY) was a flagship rural electrification programme launched by the Government of India in December 2014.

The scheme aimed to strengthen electricity distribution infrastructure in rural areas, improve the quality of power supply and provide electricity access to unelectrified inhabited villages.

It also focused on separating agricultural and non-agricultural electricity feeders so that electricity supply could be managed more effectively for farmers and other rural consumers.

Full Form: Deen Dayal Upadhyaya Gram Jyoti Yojana (DDUGJY).

2. Launch Date and Ministry

  • Launch year: December 2014.
  • Ministry: Ministry of Power, Government of India.
  • Nodal agency: Rural Electrification Corporation (REC).
  • Implementing agencies: State electricity distribution companies and other eligible utilities.
  • Programme type: Central Government rural electrification programme.
  • Closure date: 31 March 2022.

3. Main Objectives of DDUGJY

The scheme was designed to improve rural electricity infrastructure and make electricity distribution more reliable and efficient.

  • Electrify all inhabited unelectrified census villages.
  • Separate agricultural and non-agricultural electricity feeders.
  • Strengthen rural electricity distribution networks.
  • Improve the quality and reliability of electricity supply.
  • Reduce technical losses in rural electricity distribution.
  • Improve electricity availability for agricultural activities.
  • Strengthen the infrastructure required for rural development.
  • Support the goal of providing electricity access across rural India.

4. Major Components of the Scheme

DDUGJY included several important infrastructure development components.

  • Feeder Separation: Separation of agricultural and non-agricultural feeders to manage electricity supply more effectively.
  • Rural Distribution Infrastructure: Strengthening and augmentation of sub-transmission and distribution networks.
  • Village Electrification: Providing electricity infrastructure to inhabited unelectrified census villages.
  • System Metering: Installation of meters at distribution transformers, feeders and consumers.
  • Infrastructure Improvement: Construction and improvement of substations, transformers and electricity lines.

5. What is Feeder Separation?

Feeder separation means creating separate electricity distribution lines for agricultural and non-agricultural consumers.

In many rural areas, electricity is used for agricultural pumps as well as households, schools, shops and other non-agricultural activities.

  • Separate feeders help manage electricity supply to different consumer categories.
  • Farmers can receive electricity according to agricultural supply schedules.
  • Non-agricultural consumers can receive more predictable electricity supply.
  • It helps distribution companies monitor and manage electricity consumption.
Example: A village may have one feeder for agricultural pump connections and another for households, schools and shops.

6. What is Rural Distribution Infrastructure?

Rural distribution infrastructure refers to the electricity network used to deliver power from substations to villages, homes, farms and businesses.

DDUGJY supported the construction and improvement of this infrastructure.

  • Construction of new substations.
  • Improvement and augmentation of existing substations.
  • Installation of distribution transformers.
  • Construction and strengthening of high-tension and low-tension lines.
  • Improvement of electricity distribution networks.
  • Installation of meters at different levels of the distribution system.

7. Who Benefited from DDUGJY?

The programme primarily focused on rural areas and electricity distribution infrastructure.

  • Residents of inhabited unelectrified villages.
  • Rural households covered under village electrification projects.
  • Farmers and agricultural consumers.
  • Rural shops and small businesses.
  • Schools and other public institutions.
  • Electricity distribution utilities serving rural areas.
DDUGJY mainly developed village-level electricity infrastructure. It should not be confused with a universal free household connection scheme.

8. Difference Between Village Electrification and Household Electrification

One important feature of DDUGJY was its focus on village electrification. However, an electrified village does not automatically mean that every individual household has an electricity connection.

  • Village electrification: Establishing the required electricity infrastructure and meeting the prescribed government definition for an electrified village.
  • Household electrification: Providing an individual electricity connection to a household.
  • DDUGJY: Focused on village electrification and rural distribution infrastructure.
  • Saubhagya: Later focused on providing connections to willing unelectrified households.
Remember this distinction for competitive examinations: village electrification and household electrification are two different concepts.

9. Financial Outlay of DDUGJY

According to the Government's performance audit information, the approved combined outlay for DDUGJY and the subsumed RGGVY works was ₹75,893 crore, including budgetary support of ₹63,027 crore.

  • Approved combined outlay: ₹75,893 crore.
  • Approved budgetary support: ₹63,027 crore.
  • Reported final cost: ₹64,495 crore.
  • Reported budgetary support in final cost: ₹45,025 crore.
These figures relate to the combined DDUGJY and subsumed RGGVY programme expenditure reported in the audit. They are not a direct household subsidy amount.

10. Funding and Financial Assistance

DDUGJY was implemented through approved infrastructure projects submitted by eligible electricity distribution utilities and state agencies.

  • Central Government financial support was provided for approved projects.
  • State governments and distribution utilities also contributed to project implementation.
  • Institutional loans formed part of the funding structure under the prescribed guidelines.
  • Additional grants could be linked to the achievement of specified milestones and conditions.
  • Funds were released in instalments based on project progress and compliance with scheme conditions.
DDUGJY did not provide a general cash subsidy directly to households. Its financial assistance was intended for eligible electricity infrastructure projects.

11. What Was the Role of REC?

Rural Electrification Corporation (REC) was appointed as the nodal agency for DDUGJY.

  • Coordinating scheme implementation.
  • Receiving and processing project proposals.
  • Monitoring project execution.
  • Releasing funds according to approved conditions.
  • Reviewing project progress and implementation reports.
  • Supporting coordination between the Ministry of Power and implementing utilities.

12. Role of State Governments and DISCOMs

State governments and electricity distribution companies were responsible for carrying out the approved works on the ground.

  • Identifying villages requiring electrification.
  • Preparing project proposals and Detailed Project Reports (DPRs).
  • Constructing electricity distribution infrastructure.
  • Installing transformers and electricity lines.
  • Separating agricultural and non-agricultural feeders.
  • Carrying out metering and other approved works.
  • Monitoring the operation and maintenance of the network.

13. How Was the Scheme Implemented?

DDUGJY was implemented through projects approved by the Ministry of Power and carried out by state electricity distribution utilities.

  • Identification of rural electrification requirements.
  • Preparation of project proposals and cost estimates.
  • Approval of eligible infrastructure projects.
  • Release of funds according to prescribed conditions.
  • Construction of substations and distribution lines.
  • Installation of transformers and meters.
  • Completion and verification of approved works.

14. Achievements of DDUGJY

DDUGJY made a major contribution to rural electrification and the development of India's electricity distribution infrastructure.

  • Villages electrified: 18,374 inhabited census villages were reported electrified under DDUGJY.
  • Achievement date: 28 April 2018, when all inhabited unelectrified census villages were reported electrified.
  • Infrastructure: New and upgraded substations, transformers and electricity lines were developed.
  • Feeder separation: Agricultural and non-agricultural electricity supply systems were separated under approved projects.
  • Metering: Metering infrastructure was improved at different levels of the distribution system.
These are government-reported programme achievements. Village electrification does not necessarily mean that every household in each village had an electricity connection at that time.

15. Relationship with Rajiv Gandhi Grameen Vidyutikaran Yojana

DDUGJY incorporated the rural electrification component and ongoing works of the earlier Rajiv Gandhi Grameen Vidyutikaran Yojana (RGGVY).

  • RGGVY was an earlier rural electrification programme.
  • Its ongoing rural electrification works were subsumed under DDUGJY.
  • DDUGJY continued the effort to electrify rural villages.
  • It also placed greater emphasis on feeder separation and strengthening distribution infrastructure.

16. Difference Between DDUGJY and Saubhagya

Both programmes contributed to electricity access in India, but their primary objectives were different.

  • DDUGJY: Focused on rural electricity infrastructure, feeder separation and village electrification.
  • Saubhagya: Focused on providing electricity connections to willing unelectrified households.
  • DDUGJY launch: December 2014.
  • Saubhagya launch: September 2017.
  • DDUGJY nodal agency: REC.
  • Saubhagya nodal agency: REC.

17. Current Status of DDUGJY

The Government of India has confirmed that all sanctioned works under DDUGJY have been completed and the scheme stands closed.

  • Scheme status: Closed.
  • Closure date: 31 March 2022.
  • Village electrification achievement: 18,374 villages.
  • Current household electrification support: Available through separate programmes, including RDSS, subject to eligibility and approved projects.

The Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme (RDSS), launched in 2021, is a separate programme that supports improvements in electricity distribution systems. It also supports approved electrification works for identified left-out households.

DDUGJY is not currently accepting new applications. Households seeking a new electricity connection should contact their local electricity distribution company or DISCOM.

18. Benefits of Rural Electrification

  • Education: Electricity supports lighting and the use of educational devices in rural schools and homes.
  • Agriculture: Improved electricity infrastructure supports agricultural pumping and related activities.
  • Healthcare: Reliable power can support health centres and essential medical equipment.
  • Business: Electricity helps rural shops and small businesses operate equipment.
  • Communication: Electricity supports mobile charging, internet access and digital services.
  • Development: Better electricity infrastructure supports economic and social development in rural communities.

19. Important Points to Remember

  • DDUGJY stands for Deen Dayal Upadhyaya Gram Jyoti Yojana.
  • It was launched in December 2014.
  • It was implemented by the Ministry of Power.
  • REC was its nodal agency.
  • It focused on rural electrification and distribution infrastructure.
  • Feeder separation was one of its major components.
  • It included the rural electrification works of RGGVY.
  • 18,374 villages were reported electrified under the scheme.
  • All inhabited unelectrified census villages were reported electrified by 28 April 2018.
  • The scheme closed on 31 March 2022.

21. Conclusion

Deen Dayal Upadhyaya Gram Jyoti Yojana was an important step in strengthening India's rural electricity infrastructure. It improved distribution networks, supported feeder separation and helped achieve the electrification of inhabited census villages.

The programme laid an important foundation for rural electricity access. Its work was completed in March 2022, while later initiatives such as RDSS continue to support improvements in India's electricity distribution sector.
