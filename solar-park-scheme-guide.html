Solar Park Scheme

Complete Guide to Development of Solar Parks and Ultra Mega Solar Power Projects

1. What is the Solar Park Scheme?

The Development of Solar Parks and Ultra Mega Solar Power Projects Scheme is a Government of India initiative launched by the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE) in December 2014.

The scheme aims to establish large areas of land equipped with common infrastructure facilities for developing solar power projects.

These solar parks make it easier for solar power developers to establish electricity generation projects without independently arranging all basic infrastructure.

KEY IDEA: Solar parks provide developed land and shared infrastructure to encourage large-scale solar electricity generation.

2. Launch and Background

  • Launch Year: 2014.
  • Launch Date: 12 December 2014.
  • Ministry: Ministry of New and Renewable Energy.
  • Original Target: 20,000 MW of solar capacity.
  • Revised Target: 40,000 MW of solar capacity.
  • Revision Date: 21 March 2017.
  • Original Scheme Timeline: Extended up to 31 March 2026.
The scheme was introduced to overcome infrastructure and land-related difficulties faced by large-scale solar power developers.

3. Main Objectives

  • Promote large-scale solar electricity generation.
  • Provide developed land for solar projects.
  • Reduce the time required to establish solar plants.
  • Improve electricity transmission infrastructure.
  • Attract private investment in renewable energy.
  • Reduce infrastructure costs through shared facilities.
  • Encourage employment opportunities.
  • Reduce dependence on fossil fuels.
  • Support India's renewable energy development goals.

4. What is a Solar Park?

A Solar Park is a large area of land developed specifically for establishing multiple solar power generation projects.

It provides common facilities so that different solar power developers can establish their projects more efficiently.

  • Suitable land for solar installations.
  • Electricity transmission infrastructure.
  • Internal roads and connectivity.
  • Water supply facilities where required.
  • Drainage infrastructure.
  • Communication networks.
  • Required statutory clearances as applicable.

5. Major Features of the Scheme

  • The scheme supports states and union territories in developing solar parks.
  • Solar parks generally have a capacity of 500 MW or above.
  • Smaller parks may be considered in areas with difficult terrain or limited suitable land.
  • Multiple solar developers can establish projects within a park.
  • Common infrastructure helps reduce project development difficulties.
  • Solar parks support grid-connected electricity generation.
  • Implementation involves state agencies and designated developers.

6. How Does a Solar Park Work?

  • Step 1: A suitable location is identified for developing the solar park.
  • Step 2: The state government nominates an implementing agency.
  • Step 3: The proposed park is submitted for the required approvals.
  • Step 4: Land and common infrastructure are developed.
  • Step 5: Solar power developers are selected through applicable procedures.
  • Step 6: Individual solar power projects are established.
  • Step 7: Generated electricity is connected to the electricity grid.
  • Step 8: Electricity is supplied according to applicable power purchase and grid arrangements.

7. Who Can Participate?

The scheme is designed primarily for institutional and large-scale solar project development.

  • State governments.
  • State-designated renewable energy agencies.
  • Central Public Sector Undertakings (CPSUs).
  • Solar Energy Corporation of India (SECI).
  • State Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs).
  • Eligible private entrepreneurs.
  • Special Purpose Vehicles (SPVs).
IMPORTANT: Ordinary households do not apply directly for individual rooftop solar subsidies through the Solar Park Scheme.

8. Government Financial Assistance

The Ministry of New and Renewable Energy provides Central Financial Assistance (CFA) to eligible solar park development agencies under the applicable scheme guidelines.

  • DPR Preparation: Assistance of up to ₹25 lakh per solar park for preparing the Detailed Project Report.
  • Infrastructure Development: Assistance of up to ₹20 lakh per MW or 30% of the eligible project cost, whichever is lower, subject to scheme conditions.
  • Internal Infrastructure: Support may be provided for infrastructure within the solar park.
  • External Infrastructure: Support may also cover eligible transmission infrastructure outside the park.
NOTE: The exact CFA allocation depends on the selected implementation mode, eligible infrastructure, project cost and prescribed milestones. This financial assistance is not a direct household subsidy.

9. Role of Solar Energy Corporation of India (SECI)

The Solar Energy Corporation of India (SECI) plays an important role in implementing and supporting solar energy development programmes.

  • Supports solar park development.
  • Coordinates with implementing agencies.
  • Handles applicable scheme-related processes.
  • Facilitates solar project development.
  • Supports renewable energy project implementation.
  • Coordinates with government organisations and developers.

11. Major Solar Parks in India

India has developed several large-scale solar parks in different states.

  • Bhadla Solar Park: Rajasthan.
  • Pavagada Solar Park: Karnataka.
  • Kurnool Ultra Mega Solar Park: Andhra Pradesh.
  • Rewa Solar Park: Madhya Pradesh.
  • Dholera Solar Park: Gujarat.
  • Radhanesda Solar Park: Gujarat.
These projects demonstrate how large-scale solar infrastructure can support India's renewable energy development.

12. Achievements and Progress

The Solar Park Scheme has supported the development of large solar power infrastructure across India.

  • 55 solar parks had been approved.
  • Total approved capacity was approximately 39,958 MW.
  • Approvals covered 13 states.
  • Solar parks were at different stages of development and implementation.
DATA REFERENCE: These figures are based on the MNRE Annual Report 2024–25, reporting progress as of 31 December 2024. Approved capacity should not be confused with commissioned electricity generation capacity.

13. How to Avail Financial Assistance?

Financial assistance under this scheme is intended for eligible solar park development agencies, rather than individual residential consumers.

  • Step 1: The state identifies a suitable location.
  • Step 2: The state nominates a Solar Power Park Developer (SPPD).
  • Step 3: The implementing agency prepares the Detailed Project Report.
  • Step 4: The proposal is submitted to MNRE through the prescribed process.
  • Step 5: The proposal undergoes the required examination and approval.
  • Step 6: Eligible financial assistance is released according to the applicable milestones.

14. Challenges in Solar Park Development

  • Land acquisition and land availability.
  • Environmental and statutory clearances.
  • Transmission infrastructure development.
  • High initial infrastructure investment.
  • Coordination between different government agencies.
  • Availability of water in certain regions.
  • Environmental and land-use considerations.
  • Timely completion of infrastructure projects.

15. Important Things to Remember

  • Implemented by MNRE.

  • Revised target: 40 GW.
  • Supports large-scale solar power projects.
  • Generally supports parks of 500 MW and above.
  • SECI plays an important implementation role.
  • Financial assistance is subject to scheme guidelines.
  • The published scheme timeline was extended up to 31 March 2026.

16. Important Scheme Status

The published implementation timeline of the Solar Park Scheme was extended up to the end of financial year 2025–26.

Since that timeline has passed, fresh approvals, extensions or new project opportunities should be checked through the latest MNRE notifications.

WARNING: Do not assume that fresh applications or financial assistance are currently available. Verify the latest official government notifications before making project decisions.
