Atal Jyoti Yojana (AJAY)

A Government of India initiative for solar-powered street lighting

1. What is Atal Jyoti Yojana?

Atal Jyoti Yojana (AJAY) was a scheme launched by the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE) on 20 September 2016.

Its main objective was to install solar street lights in areas with inadequate street lighting, especially remote, rural and semi-urban locations.

Main Objective: To improve public lighting, safety and security through solar-powered street lights.

2. Objectives of the Scheme

  • Provide better lighting in poorly illuminated public areas.
  • Improve safety and security in streets and public spaces.
  • Promote the use of renewable solar energy.
  • Support areas with inadequate electricity infrastructure.
  • Reduce dependence on conventional electricity for street lighting.
  • Improve the quality of life in rural and semi-urban communities.

3. Launch and Development

  • 20 September 2016: AJAY Phase-I was launched.
  • December 2018: AJAY Phase-II was launched.
  • 1 April 2020: Phase-II was discontinued for new sanctions.
  • June 2022: The extended installation period for previously sanctioned Phase-II lights ended.
The scheme was implemented in two phases. Phase-II expanded coverage to additional regions and selected aspirational districts.

4. Areas Covered Under the Scheme

Phase-I:

  • Uttar Pradesh
  • Bihar
  • Jharkhand
  • Odisha
  • Assam

Phase-II:

  • States covered under Phase-I.
  • Hilly regions, including Jammu and Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand.
  • North-Eastern states, including Sikkim.
  • Island territories of Andaman and Nicobar Islands and Lakshadweep.
  • Selected parliamentary constituencies covering aspirational districts in other states.
Coverage depended on the approved scheme framework and the locations sanctioned by the authorities.

5. How Did the Scheme Work?

AJAY supported the installation of solar street lighting systems in selected public locations. These systems use sunlight to generate and store electricity for lighting at night.

  • Solar Panel: Converts sunlight into electrical energy.
  • Battery: Stores the electricity generated during the day.
  • LED Light: Provides illumination at night.
  • Controller: Helps regulate charging and lighting operation.
  • Supporting Structure: Holds the solar panel and light in position.
Solar energy is collected during daylight hours and stored for use after sunset.

6. Funding Pattern

The scheme used a shared funding arrangement involving the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy and Members of Parliament Local Area Development Scheme (MPLADS) funds.

  • 75%: Financial support from MNRE.
  • 25%: Contribution through MPLADS funds.
Important: This funding arrangement supported public street-lighting projects. It was not a direct cash subsidy for individual households.

7. Who Benefited from AJAY?

  • Residents of rural and remote communities.
  • People using poorly illuminated public roads.
  • Communities in selected semi-urban areas.
  • People living in selected aspirational districts.
  • Pedestrians and other users of public spaces.

8. Major Benefits

  • Improved visibility in public areas at night.
  • Better convenience for pedestrians and local residents.
  • Greater use of clean and renewable energy.
  • Reduced dependence on conventional electricity for the installed lights.
  • Support for lighting in areas with limited grid connectivity.
  • Potential improvement in public safety and security.

9. Achievements of the Scheme

According to Government of India information, the following installations were reported:

  • Phase-I: Approximately 1.35 lakh solar street lights installed.
  • Phase-II: Approximately 1.37 lakh solar street lights installed.
  • Total: Approximately 2.72 lakh solar street lights installed across both phases.
These figures are reported installation achievements, not the number of individual households receiving financial assistance.

10. Application and Implementation Process

AJAY was designed as a public infrastructure programme. It did not operate like an individual online application scheme for households.

  • Locations were identified for installation of solar street lights.
  • Local Members of Parliament provided consent and suggested locations under the scheme framework.
  • District Magistrates issued the required sanctions.
  • Energy Efficiency Services Limited (EESL) acted as the implementing agency.
  • Solar street lights were installed at approved public locations.
Individuals should not pay agents claiming to provide personal AJAY subsidies or guaranteed scheme registration.

11. Current Status of AJAY

AJAY Phase-II was discontinued for issuing new sanctions from 1 April 2020 after the suspension of MPLADS funding during the COVID-19 period.

In a Lok Sabha reply dated 18 December 2024, the Government stated that there was no proposal to launch AJAY Phase-III.

Important Status Update: Do not assume that fresh applications or new installations under AJAY are currently open. Check official MNRE announcements for any future government programme or replacement initiative.

12. Important Points to Remember

  • AJAY stands for Atal Jyoti Yojana.
  • It was launched on 20 September 2016.
  • It was introduced by the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy.
  • Its main focus was solar-powered public street lighting.
  • It was implemented in two phases.
  • EESL was the implementing agency.
  • It was not a direct household subsidy programme.
  • Phase-II was discontinued for new sanctions from April 2020.
  • No Phase-III proposal was reported in the December 2024 Lok Sabha reply.
