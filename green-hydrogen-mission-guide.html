National Green Hydrogen Mission

India's initiative to become a global hub for green hydrogen production and use

1. What is the National Green Hydrogen Mission?

The National Green Hydrogen Mission (NGHM) is a Government of India initiative approved in January 2023. It aims to make India a global centre for the production, use and export of green hydrogen and its derivatives.

The mission is coordinated by the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE).

Main Objective: To develop a strong green hydrogen ecosystem in India and reduce dependence on fossil fuels.

2. What is Green Hydrogen?

Green hydrogen is hydrogen produced using renewable energy, such as solar or wind power. It is commonly produced through a process called electrolysis.

During electrolysis, electricity is used to separate water into hydrogen and oxygen. When the electricity comes from renewable sources, the hydrogen produced can qualify as green hydrogen under the applicable standards.

Green hydrogen can help reduce emissions in industries where replacing fossil fuels is difficult.

3. How is Green Hydrogen Produced?

  • Renewable Energy: Solar or wind power generates electricity.
  • Water Supply: Water is supplied to the electrolysis system.
  • Electrolyser: Electricity separates water into hydrogen and oxygen.
  • Hydrogen Collection: The hydrogen is collected and processed.
  • Storage and Transport: Hydrogen is stored and transported using suitable infrastructure.
  • Final Use: It can be used in industries, transport and other applications.
Basic Process: Renewable Electricity → Electrolysis → Hydrogen + Oxygen

4. Launch and Financial Outlay

  • Approval: January 2023.
  • Nodal Ministry: Ministry of New and Renewable Energy.
  • Total Financial Outlay: ₹19,744 crore.
  • Mission Period: Up to 2029–30.
  • Production Target: At least 5 million metric tonnes of green hydrogen annually by 2030.
The mission is designed to support production, manufacturing, research, infrastructure and industrial adoption of green hydrogen.

5. Main Objectives of the Mission

  • Make India a global hub for green hydrogen production and exports.
  • Reduce dependence on imported fossil fuels.
  • Promote renewable energy-based hydrogen production.
  • Develop domestic electrolyser manufacturing.
  • Encourage research and technological innovation.
  • Support clean energy use in industries and transport.
  • Create employment and investment opportunities.
  • Reduce carbon emissions and support India's climate goals.

6. Major Components of the Mission

1. SIGHT Programme

Strategic Interventions for Green Hydrogen Transition (SIGHT) provides financial incentives for domestic electrolyser manufacturing and green hydrogen production.

2. Pilot Projects

Pilot projects test the use of green hydrogen in sectors such as steel, mobility and shipping.

3. Research and Development

Supports research into better production methods, storage, transportation and hydrogen technologies.

4. Infrastructure and Green Hydrogen Hubs

Encourages the development of facilities and infrastructure needed for large-scale production, storage and use.

5. Skill Development

Supports training and development of skilled workers for the growing green hydrogen industry.

7. What is the SIGHT Programme?

SIGHT stands for Strategic Interventions for Green Hydrogen Transition. It is a major financial support programme under the National Green Hydrogen Mission.

It has two major incentive areas:

  • Component I – Electrolyser Manufacturing: Supports domestic manufacturing of electrolysers used to produce green hydrogen.
  • Component II – Green Hydrogen Production: Provides incentives to selected producers for manufacturing green hydrogen and its derivatives.
The SIGHT Programme has a financial outlay of ₹17,490 crore under the mission.

8. Expected Outcomes by 2030

  • 5 MMT: Annual green hydrogen production capacity.
  • 125 GW: Additional renewable energy capacity associated with the mission.
  • ₹8 lakh crore: Expected total investment.
  • 6 lakh+: Expected full-time job opportunities.
  • 50 MMT: Expected annual reduction in carbon dioxide emissions.
These are mission targets and expected outcomes, not figures that have already been fully achieved.

9. Industries That Can Use Green Hydrogen

  • Steel Industry: Can use hydrogen in certain iron and steel production processes.
  • Fertiliser Industry: Green hydrogen can be used to produce green ammonia.
  • Refineries: Can replace some hydrogen currently produced from fossil fuels.
  • Transport: Can support hydrogen-powered buses, trucks and other vehicles.
  • Shipping: Green hydrogen derivatives can support cleaner shipping fuels.
  • Chemical Industry: Can be used as a feedstock for selected chemical processes.

10. Green Hydrogen Hubs

Green Hydrogen Hubs are areas where production, storage, transportation and use of green hydrogen can be developed together.

These hubs aim to bring industries and supporting infrastructure closer together, helping reduce costs and improve supply chains.

  • Support large-scale hydrogen production.
  • Encourage industrial use of green hydrogen.
  • Improve storage and transportation facilities.
  • Support exports and domestic supply chains.
The government has identified major port locations, including Kandla, Paradip and Tuticorin, as Green Hydrogen Hubs.

11. Benefits of the Mission

  • Promotes clean and renewable energy.
  • Helps reduce carbon emissions from industrial activities.
  • Encourages domestic manufacturing and innovation.
  • Can reduce dependence on imported fossil fuels.
  • Creates opportunities for investment and employment.
  • Supports India's long-term energy transition.
  • Creates opportunities for green hydrogen exports.

12. Progress of the Mission

The mission has moved from policy development towards project awards, manufacturing and commissioning.

  • Electrolyser Manufacturing: Incentives have been awarded to 15 companies for a combined manufacturing capacity of 3,000 MW per year.
  • Green Hydrogen Production: Incentives have been awarded for a cumulative production capacity of 8,62,000 tonnes per year.
  • Commissioned Capacity: Around 8,000 tonnes per year of green hydrogen production capacity had been commissioned by February 2026.
  • Certification: The Green Hydrogen Certification Portal of India was launched in June 2026 to support transparency and certification.
Progress figures are dated government-reported figures. They should not be confused with the mission's 2030 targets.

13. Who Can Participate?

The mission primarily supports organisations and institutions involved in developing the green hydrogen ecosystem.

  • Green hydrogen production companies.
  • Electrolyser manufacturers.
  • Renewable energy developers.
  • Industrial companies using hydrogen.
  • Research institutions and technology developers.
  • Start-ups working on hydrogen-related technologies.
  • Organisations involved in storage, transport and infrastructure.
This is not a general cash-benefit scheme for individual citizens. Financial support is provided through specific programmes, approved projects and competitive selection processes.

14. Application and Selection Process

There is no single application form for every component of the National Green Hydrogen Mission. Participation depends on the particular programme or funding opportunity.

  • Visit the official National Green Hydrogen Mission portal.
  • Check the available scheme guidelines and announcements.
  • Identify the relevant programme, such as SIGHT, R&D or pilot projects.
  • Review the eligibility requirements and application conditions.
  • Submit the required proposal or bid through the designated agency.
  • Applications are evaluated according to the relevant programme guidelines.
Application windows, eligibility conditions and available incentives vary by programme. Always check the latest official notification before applying.

15. Challenges in Green Hydrogen Development

  • High initial investment in production facilities.
  • Cost of renewable electricity and electrolysers.
  • Need for specialised storage and transport infrastructure.
  • Requirement for trained technical workers.
  • Need for reliable water and renewable energy supplies.
  • Developing safety standards and certification systems.
  • Making green hydrogen cost-competitive with conventional fuels.

16. Important Points to Remember
