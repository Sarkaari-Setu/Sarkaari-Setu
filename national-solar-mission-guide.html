National Solar Mission

Complete Guide to Jawaharlal Nehru National Solar Mission (JNNSM)

1. What is the National Solar Mission?

The Jawaharlal Nehru National Solar Mission (JNNSM), also known as the National Solar Mission, is a major initiative launched by the Government of India on 11 January 2010.

It operates under the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE) and aims to promote the development and large-scale use of solar energy throughout India.

The mission was introduced to make India a global leader in solar energy while supporting sustainable development and energy security.

KEY IDEA: The National Solar Mission aims to make solar energy an important part of India's electricity and energy infrastructure.

2. Launch and Background

  • Official Name: Jawaharlal Nehru National Solar Mission.
  • Launch Date: 11 January 2010.
  • Ministry: Ministry of New and Renewable Energy.
  • Coverage: All India.
  • Brand Name: Solar India.
  • Original Target: 20,000 MW of grid-connected solar power by 2022.
  • Revised Target: 100,000 MW of grid-connected solar power by 2022.
The original grid-connected solar target was increased from 20 GW to 100 GW in June 2015.

3. Main Objectives

  • Establish India as a global leader in solar energy.
  • Promote large-scale solar electricity generation.
  • Encourage the development of solar technologies.
  • Reduce dependence on fossil fuels.
  • Improve India's energy security.
  • Encourage domestic solar manufacturing.
  • Promote research and technological innovation.
  • Support sustainable and environmentally friendly development.

4. Three Phases of the National Solar Mission

The original mission was divided into three phases to support the gradual expansion of solar energy across India.


Phase I (2010–2013)

The first phase focused on establishing the foundation for solar energy development.

  • Promoted initial grid-connected solar projects.
  • Encouraged off-grid solar applications.
  • Supported solar lighting systems.
  • Encouraged early solar technology development.

Phase II (2013–2017)

The second phase focused on expanding solar electricity generation and encouraging wider adoption of solar technology.

  • Increased solar power installation capacity.
  • Promoted large-scale solar projects.
  • Encouraged solar parks.
  • Supported rooftop and decentralised solar systems.
  • Focused on reducing solar electricity costs.

Phase III (2017–2022)

The third phase focused on achieving the revised 100 GW grid-connected solar capacity target.

  • Encouraged large solar power plants.
  • Expanded rooftop solar installations.
  • Promoted solar parks and infrastructure.
  • Encouraged private sector investment.
  • Supported wider solar energy adoption.
NOTE: These are the historical implementation phases. The original Phase III timeline ended in 2022; solar development continues through subsequent government programmes and policies.

5. Major Areas Covered by the Mission

  • Grid-Connected Solar: Large solar power plants connected to the electricity distribution and transmission network.
  • Rooftop Solar: Solar panels installed on residential, commercial and institutional buildings.
  • Off-Grid Solar: Solar systems operating independently of the main electricity grid.
  • Solar Thermal: Solar energy used for heating water and other thermal applications.
  • Solar Manufacturing: Development of domestic manufacturing capabilities for solar equipment.
  • Research and Development: Improvement in solar technology, efficiency and energy storage.

6. Major Achievements

  • Helped establish a national framework for solar energy development.
  • Encouraged large-scale solar power generation.
  • Supported the growth of rooftop solar systems.
  • Promoted the development of solar parks.
  • Encouraged investment in renewable energy.
  • Supported domestic solar equipment manufacturing.
  • Helped expand solar energy access in rural and remote areas.
PROGRESS: According to the Government's National Action Plan on Climate Change dashboard, India's grid-connected solar capacity reached approximately 150.26 GW by 31 March 2026.

7. Role of MNRE

The Ministry of New and Renewable Energy is the central government ministry responsible for promoting renewable energy development in India.

  • Formulates renewable energy policies.
  • Coordinates solar energy programmes.
  • Supports research and development.
  • Promotes solar power infrastructure.
  • Coordinates with state governments and agencies.
  • Monitors renewable energy development.

8. Important Government Programmes Related to Solar Energy

Several government programmes contribute to India's wider solar energy development goals.

  • PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana: Encourages residential rooftop solar installations.
  • PM-KUSUM: Promotes solar energy in the agricultural sector.
  • Solar Parks Scheme: Supports the development of large-scale solar power project infrastructure.
  • Production Linked Incentive (PLI) Scheme: Supports domestic manufacturing of high-efficiency solar PV modules.
  • Off-Grid Solar Programmes: Encourage solar applications in areas with limited grid access.

9. Benefits of the National Solar Mission

  • Promotes clean and renewable electricity.
  • Reduces dependence on conventional energy sources.
  • Encourages technological development.
  • Creates opportunities in the solar energy sector.
  • Supports India's energy security.
  • Encourages domestic manufacturing.
  • Helps reduce greenhouse gas emissions.
  • Supports sustainable economic development.

10. Challenges in Solar Energy Development

  • High initial investment requirements.
  • Availability of suitable land for large solar projects.
  • Intermittent electricity generation due to weather and nighttime.
  • Need for efficient electricity storage systems.
  • Transmission and distribution infrastructure requirements.
  • Dependence on imported components in parts of the supply chain.
  • Need for skilled workers and technical expertise.

11. Is There an Individual Application Process?

The National Solar Mission is a national policy framework rather than a single direct-benefit application scheme for individuals.

Citizens, farmers and businesses can participate through specific solar programmes and schemes introduced under India's broader renewable energy framework.

  • Residential consumers can explore PM Surya Ghar.
  • Farmers can explore PM-KUSUM.
  • Businesses can explore applicable solar project policies.
  • Solar manufacturers can explore applicable manufacturing support schemes.

12. India's Solar Energy Goals

India's solar energy development is now connected with its broader renewable energy and climate commitments.

  • Expand solar electricity generation capacity.
  • Increase the adoption of rooftop solar.
  • Promote renewable energy in agriculture.
  • Improve domestic solar manufacturing capabilities.
  • Strengthen electricity transmission and storage infrastructure.
  • Contribute towards the national goal of 500 GW non-fossil fuel-based installed electricity capacity by 2030.
The 500 GW target refers to non-fossil fuel-based electricity capacity, not solar capacity alone.

13. Important Things to Remember

  • Launched on 11 January 2010.
  • Originally known as JNNSM.
  • Implemented under MNRE.
  • Original grid-connected target: 20 GW by 2022.
  • Revised grid-connected target: 100 GW by 2022.
  • Original mission had three implementation phases.
  • Promotes grid-connected, rooftop and off-grid solar energy.
  • It is a national mission, not a direct individual subsidy scheme.

14. Conclusion

The National Solar Mission has played an important role in transforming India's renewable energy sector. By encouraging solar power generation, technological innovation, domestic manufacturing and decentralised energy systems, it has helped establish a strong foundation for India's clean energy transition.
