GOBARdhan Scheme

Turning organic waste into clean energy, organic manure and rural prosperity

1. What is GOBARdhan?

GOBARdhan stands for Galvanizing Organic Bio-Agro Resources Dhan. It is a Government of India initiative that aims to convert biodegradable waste into useful resources such as biogas, Compressed Biogas (CBG) and organic manure.

The initiative was launched in 2018 under the Swachh Bharat Mission (Grameen) to improve village cleanliness and promote scientific management of organic waste.

Main Objective: To convert organic waste into useful products, improve sanitation and support a circular bioeconomy.

2. Meaning of GOBARdhan

The full form of GOBARdhan is:

Galvanizing Organic Bio- Agro Resources Dhan

The name reflects the idea of treating organic waste as a valuable resource instead of allowing it to accumulate and cause pollution.

3. Launch and Development

  • February 2018: GOBARdhan was announced in the Union Budget.
  • 30 April 2018: The initiative was launched under the Department of Drinking Water and Sanitation.
  • 2020: It became an integral component of Solid and Liquid Waste Management under Swachh Bharat Mission (Grameen) Phase II.
  • June 2023: The Unified GOBARdhan Portal was launched to facilitate registration of biogas and CBG plants.
  • 6 August 2026: The Union Cabinet approved GOBARdhan as the National Circular Bioenergy Scheme.
  • 1 October 2026: The new Central Sector Scheme was officially launched.
GOBARdhan has evolved from a rural waste-management initiative into a wider national programme for bioenergy development.

4. Objectives of the Scheme

  • Promote scientific management of biodegradable waste.
  • Convert cattle dung and agricultural waste into useful energy.
  • Encourage production of biogas and Compressed Biogas.
  • Produce organic manure for agricultural use.
  • Improve cleanliness and sanitation in villages.
  • Support farmers and rural entrepreneurs.
  • Promote renewable energy and reduce dependence on imported fuels.
  • Create employment opportunities in waste management and bioenergy.
  • Develop a circular economy by reusing organic resources.

5. Types of Waste Used

GOBARdhan promotes the use of different types of biodegradable waste as raw materials.

  • Cattle Dung: Waste collected from cows, buffaloes and other livestock.
  • Agricultural Residue: Organic leftovers from farming activities.
  • Kitchen Waste: Biodegradable food scraps and vegetable waste.
  • Market Waste: Organic waste generated by vegetable and fruit markets.
  • Food Waste: Suitable biodegradable waste from institutions and other sources.
  • Press Mud: Organic residue generated by sugar mills.
  • Other Biomass: Suitable biodegradable materials that can be processed in biogas plants.
Only suitable biodegradable materials should be used in a plant. Plastic, metals and other non-biodegradable contaminants need to be removed.

6. How Does GOBARdhan Work?

  • Collection: Organic waste is collected from farms, households, markets and other sources.
  • Segregation: Non-biodegradable materials and unsuitable contaminants are removed.
  • Processing: Suitable organic waste is fed into a biogas plant.
  • Anaerobic Digestion: Microorganisms break down organic matter in an oxygen-free environment.
  • Biogas Production: The process produces a gas mixture containing methane and carbon dioxide.
  • Gas Treatment: The gas can be cleaned and upgraded for suitable applications.
  • Manure Production: The remaining digestate can be processed into organic manure.
Organic Waste → Biogas Plant → Biogas + Organic Manure

7. What is Biogas?

Biogas is a renewable gas produced when microorganisms break down biodegradable organic matter in the absence of oxygen.

It mainly contains methane and carbon dioxide, along with small quantities of other gases.

  • Can be used for cooking in suitable systems.
  • Can be used for heating.
  • Can generate electricity using suitable equipment.
  • Can be upgraded to produce Compressed Biogas.

8. What is Compressed Biogas (CBG)?

Compressed Biogas is produced by cleaning and upgrading biogas to remove unwanted components and increase its methane concentration.

The upgraded gas is then compressed for storage and transportation. It can be used as a fuel in suitable vehicles, industrial applications and gas distribution systems.

  • Can be used as a transport fuel in suitable vehicles.
  • Can be supplied to industries.
  • Can be used in compatible gas distribution systems.
  • Can help replace some conventional fossil-based fuels.
CBG is a purified and compressed form of biogas. It is different from untreated biogas produced directly inside a digester.

9. What Happens to the Waste After Gas Production?

The material remaining after anaerobic digestion is called digestate or slurry.

  • It can be processed into Fermented Organic Manure (FOM).
  • It can be processed into Liquid Fermented Organic Manure (LFOM).
  • Suitable processed material can be used as an organic soil input.
  • It helps recover nutrients from biodegradable waste.
GOBARdhan supports both energy recovery and the productive use of organic by-products.

10. Main Types of GOBARdhan Projects

1. Household Biogas Plants

Small-scale plants that process household or livestock waste to produce biogas for local use.

2. Community Biogas Plants

Plants that collect organic waste from several households or a village community.

3. Cluster-Based Biogas Plants

Larger facilities that process waste collected from multiple villages or nearby locations.

4. Compressed Biogas Plants

Larger commercial facilities that produce, upgrade and compress biogas for wider use.

5. Municipal Organic Waste Projects

Facilities that process suitable biodegradable municipal waste to produce biogas or CBG.

11. Financial Assistance Under the Original Rural Programme

Under Swachh Bharat Mission (Grameen) Phase II, financial assistance of up to ₹50 lakh per district was available for setting up community or cluster-based GOBARdhan projects.

  • Support was intended for eligible district-level projects.
  • Community and cluster-based biogas plants could be established.
  • Projects were expected to have a reliable supply of organic waste.
  • Funding was subject to approved plans, guidelines and budget availability.
This ₹50 lakh assistance relates to the SBM(G) Phase II community/cluster project framework. It should not be confused with the financial support under the new National Circular Bioenergy Scheme.

12. National Circular Bioenergy Scheme – 2026

On 6 August 2026, the Union Cabinet approved GOBARdhan as India's National Circular Bioenergy Scheme. It was officially launched on 1 October 2026.

  • Total Outlay: ₹23,731 crore.
  • Implementation Period: FY 2026–27 to FY 2035–36.
  • Nodal Ministry: Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas.
  • Main Focus: Development and expansion of the Compressed Biogas sector.
  • Production Ambition: Increase domestic CBG production nearly ten-fold, to around 5 MMSCMD.
The new scheme brings multiple support measures together under a unified framework to accelerate CBG production and investment.

13. Support Under the New Scheme

The National Circular Bioenergy Scheme is designed to address several challenges faced by CBG producers and project developers.

  • Assured Offtake: Support for reliable demand and purchase arrangements for CBG.
  • Stable Pricing: A more predictable pricing framework for producers.
  • Capital Assistance: Financial support for eligible project development.
  • Pipeline Connectivity: Support for infrastructure needed to connect CBG facilities with gas networks.
  • Credit Support: Measures to improve access to finance.
  • Technology Development: Support for improved production and processing systems.
  • Ecosystem Development: Coordination between producers, buyers, financial institutions and government agencies.
Exact eligibility, incentive amounts and application conditions must be checked in the latest official scheme guidelines and notifications.

14. Expected Outcomes of the New Scheme

  • Increase domestic CBG production nearly ten-fold.
  • Reach around 5 MMSCMD of CBG production.
  • Encourage private investment in bioenergy projects.
  • Strengthen India's domestic energy supply.
  • Create new opportunities for farmers and rural entrepreneurs.
  • Promote organic waste collection and processing.
  • Expand the production and use of organic manure.
These are expected outcomes and targets of the new scheme, not figures that have already been fully achieved.

15. Who Can Participate?

  • Farmers and farmer groups.
  • Farmer Producer Organisations (FPOs).
  • Cooperative societies.
  • Village-level organisations and local bodies.
  • Gaushalas and dairy-related organisations.
  • Private companies and CBG project developers.
  • Waste-management organisations.
  • Industrial and institutional waste generators.
  • Technology providers and financial institutions.
Participation does not automatically guarantee financial assistance. Each programme has its own eligibility and approval requirements.

16. How to Register a Biogas or CBG Plant

The Government has developed a Unified GOBARdhan Portal to facilitate registration of biogas and CBG plants.

  • Visit the official GOBARdhan registration portal.
  • Review the registration instructions and applicable requirements.
  • Provide the required project and organisation details.
  • Submit the relevant information and supporting documents.
  • Complete the verification process as required.
  • Obtain the registration number after successful registration.
Registration helps identify and track plants. It does not automatically mean that a project has received financial assistance or approval under every government scheme.

17. Role of Government Departments

  • Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas: Leads the National Circular Bioenergy Scheme and supports the CBG ecosystem.
  • Ministry of Jal Shakti: Supports GOBARdhan's rural sanitation and waste-management activities under SBM(G).
  • Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs: Supports relevant urban waste-management and waste-to-energy initiatives.
  • State Governments: Coordinate implementation and support eligible projects within their jurisdictions.
  • Gram Panchayats and Local Bodies: Help organise waste collection and local project implementation.

18. Benefits of the Scheme

  • Improves the management of biodegradable waste.
  • Promotes cleaner villages and public spaces.
  • Produces renewable biogas and CBG.
  • Creates useful organic manure from digestate.
  • Can provide additional income opportunities for farmers.
  • Encourages rural entrepreneurship and employment.
  • Supports India's energy security.
  • Encourages private investment in bioenergy.
  • Promotes a circular economy.

19. Environmental Benefits

  • Reduces unmanaged accumulation of organic waste.
  • Encourages scientific treatment of animal and agricultural waste.
  • Can reduce methane emissions from unmanaged organic waste when properly designed and operated.
  • Supports renewable fuel production.
  • Promotes nutrient recovery through organic manure.
  • Can reduce dependence on some conventional fossil fuels.
The environmental benefits depend on proper waste collection, plant operation, gas leakage prevention and responsible digestate management.

20. Current Progress and Status

According to the Government's August 2026 information:

  • Registered Plants: 1,908 CBG/Bio-CNG plants as of 6 August 2026.
  • Commissioned Plants: 217 plants.
  • Plants Under Construction: 339 plants.
  • Production: Commissioned plants were reported to produce around 0.4 MMSCMD.

Separately, the Ministry of Jal Shakti reported functional CBG and community biogas projects through the rural GOBARdhan programme as of 27 January 2026.

Important: These figures come from different government reporting dates and categories. Registered, functional, commissioned and under-construction plants are not interchangeable measures.

21. Challenges in Implementation

  • Ensuring a continuous supply of organic waste.
  • Managing collection and transportation costs.
  • High initial investment in large CBG plants.
  • Need for reliable gas upgrading and storage technology.
  • Developing suitable pipeline and distribution infrastructure.
  • Maintaining consistent quality of organic manure.
  • Ensuring reliable buyers and long-term demand.
  • Training skilled workers for plant operation and maintenance.
