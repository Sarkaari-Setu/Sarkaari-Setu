Unnat Chulha Abhiyan

A Government of India initiative promoting improved biomass cookstoves and cleaner cooking solutions

1. What is Unnat Chulha Abhiyan?

Unnat Chulha Abhiyan (UCA) was a programme launched by the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE) in June 2014. Its main purpose was to promote improved biomass cookstoves that could provide more efficient and cleaner cooking compared with traditional chulhas.

The programme focused on households and community kitchens that depended on solid biomass fuels such as firewood, agricultural residue and other suitable biomass materials.

Important: Unnat Chulha Abhiyan is a historical government programme. Do not confuse it with a currently open household subsidy scheme.

2. When Was It Launched?

  • Launch year: 2014
  • Launch date: 27 June 2014, according to programme records.
  • Ministry: Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE).
  • Programme type: Central Sector Programme.
  • Focus: Development and deployment of improved biomass cookstoves.

3. Main Objectives of the Scheme

The programme aimed to improve cooking conditions and reduce the environmental impact of traditional biomass cooking.

  • Promote improved biomass cookstoves in rural, semi-urban and urban areas.
  • Reduce the amount of firewood and other biomass required for cooking.
  • Encourage more efficient use of available cooking fuel.
  • Reduce smoke and harmful emissions from traditional cooking practices.
  • Reduce the difficulties faced by women and children involved in traditional cooking activities.
  • Help reduce black carbon and other emissions associated with biomass burning.
  • Support cleaner and more sustainable cooking-energy solutions.

4. What is an Unnat Chulha?

An Unnat Chulha is an improved biomass cookstove designed to use biomass fuel more efficiently than a traditional open or basic chulha.

Depending on its design, an improved cookstove can improve combustion and direct heat towards the cooking vessel. Some models also include arrangements to direct smoke outside the cooking area.

  • Designed for improved fuel efficiency.
  • Can reduce fuel consumption compared with inefficient traditional stoves.
  • May produce lower emissions when properly designed, installed and operated.
  • Can be developed for household or community cooking requirements.
Remember: Improved biomass cookstoves still burn biomass. They should not be described as completely pollution-free or equivalent to electric or other genuinely clean cooking technologies.

5. Who Could Benefit?

The programme was intended to support people and institutions that relied on biomass for cooking, particularly in areas where access to cleaner cooking alternatives was limited.

  • Rural households using traditional biomass chulhas.
  • Community kitchens.
  • Mid-Day Meal kitchens in schools.
  • Anganwadi centres.
  • Tribal hostels and other institutional kitchens.
  • Small food businesses and roadside dhabas.
  • Other eligible community and institutional users.

6. Types of Improved Cookstoves

The programme supported improved biomass cookstoves for different cooking requirements.

  • Family-type cookstoves: Designed for household cooking needs.
  • Community-type cookstoves: Designed for larger kitchens serving groups of people.
  • Institutional cookstoves: Suitable for kitchens in schools, hostels, Anganwadi centres and similar establishments.
The appropriate stove depends on the number of people served, the type of biomass fuel available and the kitchen's requirements.

7. Financial Assistance and Subsidy

Historical programme documents describe financial support for the development and distribution of improved biomass cookstoves through approved projects and implementing organisations.

A NITI Aayog-hosted clean cooking report refers to historical UCA subsidy support in the range of approximately ₹300 to ₹800 per improved cookstove. This is a historical reference, not a confirmed current subsidy rate.

  • Support was linked to programme implementation and eligible projects.
  • Distribution could involve implementing agencies and other approved organisations.
  • Beneficiary contribution and project costs could vary.
  • No current universal household subsidy amount has been verified.
Important: Do not assume that a household can currently claim ₹300–₹800 or apply for a free chulha under this programme. Confirm any current assistance directly with MNRE or the relevant State Nodal Agency.

8. How Was the Programme Implemented?

The programme used implementing organisations to help develop, demonstrate and distribute improved cookstoves. Its implementation was not limited to a single online application portal.

  • State Nodal Agencies and relevant state departments.
  • Research and academic institutions.
  • Self-Help Groups and their federations.
  • Non-governmental and community-based organisations.
  • Eligible manufacturers and project developers.
  • Departments responsible for institutional kitchens and community facilities.

Activities could include beneficiary identification, demonstrations, training, distribution and monitoring of installed cookstoves.

9. How to Apply or Enquire?

No currently open nationwide individual application process for Unnat Chulha Abhiyan has been verified. The programme's historical implementation involved approved agencies and project-based distribution.

If you want to enquire about any ongoing improved cookstove initiative, follow these steps:

  • Visit the official MNRE website.
  • Check its current programmes and notifications.
  • Contact the State Nodal Agency responsible for renewable energy in your state.
  • Ask whether any current improved biomass cookstove project is operating in your district.
  • Confirm eligibility, availability, cost and any financial assistance directly with the authorised agency.
Avoid websites or agents promising guaranteed free chulhas or asking for unofficial registration fees in the name of this historical programme.

10. Documents That May Be Requested

Requirements depend on the current project and the implementing agency. For any new distribution initiative, the agency may request documents such as:

  • Identity proof.
  • Address or residence proof.
  • Basic household or beneficiary information.
  • Institutional details, if applying on behalf of a school or community kitchen.
  • Any additional documents specified by the authorised implementing agency.
This is an indicative list, not an official current Unnat Chulha Abhiyan application checklist.

11. Achievements of the Programme

Government records reported the distribution of improved cookstoves under the programme.

  • By March 2018, 36,940 family-type improved cookstoves had been distributed.
  • By March 2018, 849 community-type improved cookstoves had been distributed.
  • An MNRE Annual Report for 2018–19 later recorded approximately 51,641 improved biomass cookstoves deployed, including 50,322 family-type and 1,319 community-type units.
These are historical reported figures and should not be interpreted as the number of stoves currently in use.

12. Benefits of Improved Biomass Cookstoves

  • Fuel efficiency: Improved designs can reduce the amount of biomass needed for cooking.
  • Reduced smoke: Properly designed stoves can reduce smoke and emissions compared with traditional chulhas.
  • Convenience: Some designs make cooking easier and improve heat control.
  • Environmental benefits: More efficient fuel use can reduce pressure on fuelwood resources.
  • Community support: Larger cookstove designs can serve schools and other community kitchens.

13. Difference Between Traditional and Improved Chulha

  • Traditional chulha: Often has a basic design and can burn fuel inefficiently.
  • Improved chulha: Uses an improved combustion and heat-transfer design to make better use of biomass.
  • Fuel use: Improved models may require less fuel for the same cooking task.
  • Emissions: Improved models can reduce emissions, although performance varies by design and use.
  • Maintenance: Both types require proper operation and maintenance.

14. Important Points to Remember

  • Unnat Chulha Abhiyan was launched in June 2014.
  • It was implemented by the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy.
  • Its main focus was improved biomass cookstoves.
  • It aimed to promote efficient cooking and reduce smoke and emissions.
  • It covered household as well as community and institutional cooking needs.
  • Historical subsidy references should not be treated as current benefits.
  • No current nationwide individual application window has been verified.

16. Conclusion

Unnat Chulha Abhiyan was an initiative to improve cooking conditions through more efficient biomass cookstoves. It focused on reducing fuel consumption, lowering emissions and supporting households and community kitchens that depended on traditional biomass cooking.

Although the programme is important in India's clean cooking policy history, people should verify the availability of any current assistance through official government channels before applying or making payments.
