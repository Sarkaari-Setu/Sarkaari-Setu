Waste-to-Energy Programme

Complete Guide to Converting Waste into Renewable Energy in India

1. What is the Waste-to-Energy Programme?

The Waste-to-Energy (WtE) Programme is an initiative of the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE), Government of India. It promotes projects that convert urban, industrial and agricultural waste or residues into useful energy.

Depending on the type of waste and technology used, these projects can generate:

  • Biogas.
  • Bio-CNG and Compressed Biogas (CBG).
  • Electricity.
  • Producer gas and syngas.
  • Thermal energy for industrial applications.

The programme provides Central Financial Assistance (CFA) to eligible project developers for setting up and commissioning approved Waste-to-Energy projects.

Implementing Ministry: Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE), Government of India.

2. Objectives of the Programme

  • Waste Management: Encourage the scientific treatment and productive use of urban, industrial and agricultural waste.
  • Renewable Energy: Generate electricity, biogas and other useful energy from waste.
  • Clean Fuel: Promote Bio-CNG and other renewable fuels as alternatives to conventional fuels.
  • Industrial Development: Encourage industries to use waste and residues for energy production.
  • Environmental Protection: Help reduce unmanaged waste disposal and associated pollution.
  • Energy Security: Increase the contribution of locally available resources to India's energy supply.
  • Investment: Encourage private-sector participation in renewable energy and waste-processing projects.

3. Which Types of Waste Can Be Used?

The programme supports projects using different categories of biodegradable waste, industrial residues and municipal waste, subject to the applicable technology and eligibility guidelines.

A. Urban Waste

  • Vegetable and fruit market waste.
  • Kitchen and food waste.
  • Segregated municipal organic waste.
  • Slaughterhouse waste.
  • Sewage and suitable sewage-treatment residues.

B. Agricultural Waste

  • Paddy straw and other suitable crop residues.
  • Agricultural processing waste.
  • Animal dung and livestock waste.
  • Poultry waste.
  • Green grass and other suitable biodegradable material.

C. Industrial Waste

  • Agro-processing industry residues.
  • Paper and pulp industry waste.
  • Milk-processing waste.
  • Sugar industry residues and effluents.
  • Other eligible biodegradable industrial waste and effluents.

D. Municipal Solid Waste

  • Eligible municipal solid waste streams.
  • Waste suitable for approved energy-recovery technologies.
Not all waste is suitable for every technology. Waste segregation, composition, moisture content and contamination must be assessed before selecting a project design.

4. Technologies Supported

A. Biomethanation

Biomethanation is a process in which microorganisms break down biodegradable organic waste in an oxygen-free environment. It produces biogas, which can be used for energy generation or upgraded into Bio-CNG.

B. Combustion

Suitable waste or processed fuel is burned under controlled conditions to produce heat. The heat can be used to generate steam and electricity.

C. Gasification

Gasification converts suitable biomass and organic residues into a combustible gas mixture called producer gas or syngas under controlled conditions.

D. Pyrolysis

Pyrolysis uses heat in an oxygen-limited environment to convert suitable waste materials into products such as gas, liquid and solid residues.

E. Combined Technologies

Projects may use a combination of approved technologies depending on the composition of the waste and the intended energy output.

The selected technology must comply with MNRE guidelines and applicable environmental and technical regulations.

5. Main Categories of Projects

  • Biogas Production Projects: Convert biodegradable urban, agricultural and industrial waste into biogas.
  • Bio-CNG Projects: Upgrade biogas into enriched or compressed biogas for suitable fuel applications.
  • Power Generation Projects: Use biogas or eligible waste-based technologies to generate electricity.
  • Municipal Waste-to-Energy Projects: Recover energy from eligible municipal solid waste through approved technologies.
  • Biomass Gasifier Projects: Use biomass and agricultural residues to provide electricity or thermal energy to industries and other users.
The programme is designed mainly for project developers, industries, institutions and eligible organisations. It is not a general household cash-subsidy scheme.

6. Financial Assistance for Bio-CNG Projects

MNRE provides Central Financial Assistance to eligible Bio-CNG, enriched biogas and Compressed Biogas projects under the applicable Waste-to-Energy guidelines.

  • New biogas plant: ₹4 crore per 4,800 kg/day of Bio-CNG generation capacity.
  • Existing biogas plant: ₹3 crore per 4,800 kg/day of Bio-CNG generation capacity.
  • Maximum assistance: ₹10 crore per project for either category.

The assistance is subject to project eligibility, approved capacity, commissioning and performance requirements.

These are published programme rates. They do not mean that new applications are currently being accepted. Check the BioURJA Portal for the latest application status.

7. Financial Assistance for Power Generation

A. Power Generation Based on Biogas

  • ₹0.75 crore per MW for power generation from a new biogas plant.
  • ₹0.50 crore per MW for power generation from an existing biogas plant.
  • Maximum CFA: ₹5 crore per project.

B. Power from Bio and Agro-Industrial Waste

  • ₹0.40 crore per MW for eligible power-generation projects using bio and agro-industrial waste, other than municipal solid waste through incineration.
  • Maximum CFA: ₹5 crore per project.

C. Biomass Gasifier Projects

  • ₹2,500 per kWe for electrical applications using dual-fuel engines.
  • ₹15,000 per kWe for electrical applications using 100% gas engines.
  • ₹2 lakh per 300 kWth for eligible thermal applications.
The applicable assistance depends on the project category, technology, capacity and current guidelines. These figures should not be added together unless the official rules specifically permit it.

8. Additional Assistance for Special Categories

The programme provides for additional financial assistance in specified cases.

  • Projects located in eligible North-Eastern States.
  • Projects in Sikkim, Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand.
  • Projects in Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh.
  • Projects in Lakshadweep and Andaman and Nicobar Islands.
  • Eligible biomethanation projects set up in registered Gaushalas or shelters.

The applicable CFA may be 20% higher than the standard CFA for eligible categories under the programme guidelines.

Additional assistance is conditional. The project must meet the relevant location, registration and technical requirements.

9. Who Can Apply?

The programme is intended for eligible project developers and organisations planning to establish approved Waste-to-Energy facilities.

Potential project applicants may include:

  • Private-sector project developers.
  • Companies and industrial organisations.
  • Public-sector undertakings.
  • Municipal and other local government bodies.
  • Eligible government and semi-government agencies.
  • Other organisations meeting the programme's project and financial requirements.

Projects must demonstrate the availability of suitable waste, appropriate technology, financial arrangements and compliance with relevant approvals.

Eligibility is assessed at the project level. Merely owning agricultural land or producing waste does not automatically qualify an individual for MNRE Waste-to-Energy CFA.

10. Loan and Financial Requirements

Under the programme framework, projects financed through banks or financial institutions can be considered for assistance.

  • Eligible projects may obtain term loans from banks or financial institutions.
  • Self-financed projects may also be considered under the prescribed approval process.
  • For certain self-financed projects, recommendations of the Project Appraisal Committee (PAC) are mandatory.
  • Local bodies and eligible government organisations may have specific provisions regarding loan requirements.
  • The project must demonstrate its technical and financial feasibility.
The loan and approval requirements can differ by project type. Applicants should consult the applicable MNRE guidelines and implementing agency before arranging finance.

11. Application Process

Applications for financial assistance under the Waste-to-Energy Programme are processed through the BioURJA Portal.

  • Visit the official BioURJA Portal.
  • Register as a project developer, where applications are open.
  • Select the relevant Waste-to-Energy project category.
  • Submit project details, proposed capacity and technology information.
  • Provide the required financial and technical documents.
  • Submit the proposal for in-principle approval before commissioning the plant.
  • Complete the required appraisal and approval process.
  • Install and commission the project within the prescribed period.
  • Complete inspection and performance verification for eligible CFA release.
Visit BioURJA Portal
Current application status: The BioURJA Portal states that the last date for submission of Biomass and Waste-to-Energy applications was 31 December 2025. New applications have been stopped until further notice. Do not treat the portal link as confirmation that applications are open.

12. In-Principle Approval

Under the programme guidelines, project developers are required to seek in-principle approval of Central Financial Assistance before commissioning the Waste-to-Energy plant.

  • Applications are submitted through the BioURJA Portal.
  • Projects with bank or financial institution loans undergo the prescribed financial and administrative approval process.
  • Self-financed projects are examined through the implementing agency and Project Appraisal Committee, where applicable.
  • Approval is subject to the Ministry's requirements and available programme provisions.
In-principle approval is not the same as final subsidy disbursement. The project must still satisfy commissioning and performance conditions.

13. Project Commissioning and Performance

Financial assistance is linked to the successful commissioning and performance of the project.

  • Waste-to-Energy plants generally have a prescribed commissioning period of 24 months from the date of in-principle approval.
  • Biomass gasifier projects generally have a prescribed commissioning period of 12 months.
  • Performance is assessed using the prescribed operating and generation parameters.
  • Applicable inspection and monitoring requirements must be fulfilled.
  • SCADA or remote-monitoring systems are required for Waste-to-Energy projects, except biomass gasifiers, as specified in the programme guidelines.

Performance-based assistance under the published guidelines:

  • Average Plant Load Factor (PLF) of 80% or more: 100% of eligible CFA.
  • PLF of 60% to below 80%: 80% of eligible CFA.
  • PLF of 50% to below 60%: 60% of eligible CFA.
  • PLF below 50%: No CFA under this performance table.
The 2025 revised guidelines introduced provisions for staged CFA release and performance-linked disbursement. The applicable revised conditions must be checked for each approved project.

14. Revised Guidelines – June 2025

On 28 June 2025, MNRE announced revised Waste-to-Energy guidelines intended to simplify procedures and improve the release of financial assistance.

  • Provision for release of 50% of eligible CFA after obtaining the State Pollution Control Board's Consent to Operate certificate, against a bank guarantee.
  • The remaining CFA is linked to the project's performance.
  • Pro-rata disbursement provisions were introduced for projects that do not achieve the full performance threshold.
  • Procedures were revised to improve transparency and reduce delays.
  • The changes were intended to support public- and private-sector project developers.
These revisions relate to the programme's approved project framework. They do not reopen the application window automatically.

15. Documents Required

The documents required depend on the project category, technology, financing arrangement and approval stage.

  • Applicant or organisation registration documents.
  • Project proposal and Detailed Project Report (DPR).
  • Details of waste availability and supply arrangements.
  • Plant capacity and technology specifications.
  • Project cost estimates and financial arrangements.
  • Bank loan sanction or financial institution documents, where applicable.
  • Land or site-related documents.
  • Environmental and statutory approvals, as applicable.
  • Consent to Establish and Consent to Operate, where required.
  • Commissioning and performance reports.
This is a general checklist. The official application portal and current programme guidelines determine the exact documents required.

16. Benefits of Waste-to-Energy Projects

  • Convert suitable waste into useful energy.
  • Support better urban and industrial waste management.
  • Help reduce the amount of biodegradable waste sent to disposal sites.
  • Provide renewable fuel for eligible industrial and transport applications.
  • Support electricity and thermal energy generation.
  • Create opportunities for investment and employment.
  • Encourage the development of a circular economy.
  • Help improve the productive use of agricultural residues.

17. Difference Between Waste-to-Energy and GOBARdhan

Waste-to-Energy Programme:

  • Implemented by MNRE.
  • Supports eligible projects converting urban, industrial and agricultural waste into energy.
  • Can support biogas, Bio-CNG, power and gasification projects.
  • Provides CFA under the applicable programme guidelines.

GOBARdhan National Circular Bioenergy Scheme:

  • A separate national initiative focused on scaling up the Compressed Biogas ecosystem.
  • Promotes conversion of cattle dung, agricultural residues and other organic resources into CBG and organic manure.
  • Approved in August 2026 with a total outlay of ₹23,731 crore.
  • Designed for implementation from FY 2026–27 to FY 2035–36.
Both initiatives support waste-based energy, but they have different structures, objectives, implementation arrangements and eligibility conditions.

18. Latest Scheme Status – November 2026

The Waste-to-Energy Programme was part of Phase-I of the National Bioenergy Programme.

  • Phase-I covered FY 2021–22 to FY 2025–26.
  • The overall National Bioenergy Programme Phase-I had an approved outlay of ₹858 crore.
  • The BioURJA Portal states that the last date for submission of Waste-to-Energy applications was 31 December 2025.
  • The portal currently states that new applications have been stopped until further notice.
  • The government continues to report operational Waste-to-Energy capacity as part of India's renewable bioenergy sector.

According to a government update, as of 30 September 2026, grid-connected Waste-to-Energy capacity stood at 324.24 MW, while off-grid capacity stood at 554.16 MW.

Important: Published CFA rates should not be interpreted as an active application opportunity. Check for a new MNRE notification or a revised BioURJA application notice before planning a project around this assistance.
