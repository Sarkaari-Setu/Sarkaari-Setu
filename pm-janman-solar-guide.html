PM JANMAN Solar Scheme

Solar-powered electricity for remote tribal and PVTG communities

1. What is PM JANMAN?

PM JANMAN stands for Pradhan Mantri Janjati Adivasi Nyaya Maha Abhiyan. It is a Government of India initiative launched in November 2023 to improve essential facilities and living conditions in Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Group (PVTG) communities.

The mission includes interventions across different sectors, such as housing, drinking water, education, healthcare, roads and electricity.

The Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE) implements the New Solar Power Scheme under PM JANMAN to provide off-grid solar electricity in eligible tribal and PVTG habitations.

Main Objective: To provide electricity to identified un-electrified tribal and PVTG households in areas where conventional grid electricity is not technically or economically feasible.

2. What is a PVTG?

PVTG stands for Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Group. It is a category of tribal communities identified by the Government of India for focused development support because of specific socio-economic and developmental challenges.

  • These communities may live in remote or difficult-to-reach areas.
  • Some habitations have limited access to basic infrastructure.
  • Electricity and other essential services may be difficult to provide through conventional networks.
  • Government programmes aim to improve access to essential facilities.
PM JANMAN gives special attention to the development needs of PVTG communities.

3. About the New Solar Power Scheme

The New Solar Power Scheme under PM JANMAN was introduced to provide electricity through off-grid solar systems to identified un-electrified households in PVTG habitations.

  • Approved financial outlay for the original PM JANMAN solar component: ₹515 crore.
  • Target: 1 lakh un-electrified households.
  • Provision for solar lighting in 1,500 Multi-Purpose Centres (MPCs).
  • Solar systems are intended for locations where grid electricity is not techno-economically feasible.
The programme focuses on providing electricity access rather than distributing cash assistance to individual households.

4. Main Objectives

  • Provide electricity to identified un-electrified PVTG households.
  • Improve access to basic energy services in remote villages.
  • Promote solar energy in areas with limited grid connectivity.
  • Support lighting facilities in Multi-Purpose Centres.
  • Improve living conditions and access to essential services.
  • Reduce dependence on conventional electricity infrastructure in remote locations.
  • Promote clean and renewable energy.

5. Main Components of the Solar Scheme

1. Solar Home Lighting Systems

Individual off-grid solar systems are provided to scattered, identified un-electrified households. These systems include necessary appliances such as LED lights and a fan.

2. Solar Mini-Grids

Where several households are located close together, a solar mini-grid may be installed to supply electricity to a group of homes.

3. Solar Lighting for Multi-Purpose Centres

Identified Multi-Purpose Centres in PVTG areas may receive off-grid solar power systems and lighting facilities where grid electricity is not available.

The choice between an individual solar home system and a mini-grid depends on the location and implementation requirements.

6. How Does the Solar System Work?

  • Solar Panel: Captures sunlight and converts it into electricity.
  • Battery: Stores electricity for use when sunlight is unavailable.
  • Charge Controller: Regulates battery charging and protects the system.
  • LED Lights: Provide lighting inside the household or public facility.
  • Fan: Provides basic household comfort where included in the system.
  • Monitoring System: Helps track the operation and performance of the installed system.
Solar panels generate electricity during the day. The battery stores energy so that lights and other connected appliances can be used later.

7. Financial Assistance

The scheme provides Central Financial Assistance (CFA) for approved installations. The support is provided through the implementing agencies rather than as cash paid directly to households.

  • Solar Home Systems: Up to ₹50,000 per un-electrified household or the actual eligible cost, whichever is lower.
  • Solar Mini-Grids: Central assistance is limited to ₹50,000 per un-electrified household covered by the mini-grid.
  • Multi-Purpose Centres: Assistance of up to ₹1 lakh per MPC under the PM JANMAN component.
Important: These are scheme-level financial assistance limits. Eligible households are provided with approved solar systems, not a personal cash payment.

8. What is Included in the Household System?

The approved solar home lighting system is designed to provide basic electricity facilities to identified households.

  • Solar power system of the approved capacity, generally 0.3 kW.
  • Battery storage.
  • LED lighting appliances.
  • Fan and other specified appliances.
  • Necessary installation and supporting equipment.
  • Remote monitoring arrangements.
  • On-site comprehensive maintenance services for five years.
The system configuration and appliances are determined by the approved technical guidelines.

10. Who is Eligible?

The solar component is intended for identified un-electrified households in eligible tribal and PVTG habitations.

  • The household must be located in an identified eligible habitation.
  • The household must meet the scheme's un-electrified household criteria.
  • Grid electricity must not be techno-economically feasible for the location.
  • The household must be covered through the approved implementation process.
Being a tribal household alone does not automatically guarantee installation. Coverage depends on the official identification and eligibility process.

11. How to Avail the Solar System?

The scheme is implemented through designated agencies and electricity distribution companies. It is not a general public cash-subsidy application.

  • Check whether your habitation is included in the approved coverage.
  • Contact the respective electricity distribution company (DISCOM).
  • Ask about the solar electrification arrangements for your habitation.
  • The implementing agency verifies the eligible households and site requirements.
  • Approved solar home systems or mini-grids are installed.
  • Maintenance and monitoring arrangements are provided as per the scheme guidelines.
Do not pay unofficial agents who promise guaranteed PM JANMAN registration or personal cash benefits. Confirm all details with the relevant DISCOM or government authority.

12. Role of Government Departments

  • Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE): Provides scheme guidelines and Central Financial Assistance.
  • Ministry of Tribal Affairs: Identifies eligible tribal and PVTG habitations and coordinates the wider PM JANMAN mission.
  • State Governments: Support implementation and coordination within their states.
  • DISCOMs: Help implement solar electrification in the identified areas.
  • Implementing Agencies: Arrange installation, monitoring and maintenance according to the approved guidelines.

13. Benefits of the Scheme

  • Improves access to electricity in remote habitations.
  • Provides basic lighting facilities to eligible households.
  • Supports education and household activities after sunset.
  • Improves lighting in selected public facilities.
  • Promotes renewable energy use.
  • Reduces dependence on conventional grid infrastructure in difficult locations.
  • Supports the broader development of PVTG communities.
  • Provides maintenance support for installed household systems.

14. Connection with DAJGUA

In October 2024, MNRE issued revised guidelines bringing the solar programme for tribal and PVTG habitations under both PM JANMAN and the Dharti Aaba Janjatiya Gram Utkarsh Abhiyan (DAJGUA).

The revised programme continued the household electrification and Multi-Purpose Centre components under PM JANMAN and added a separate component for solarising public institutions under DAJGUA.

  • PM JANMAN: Solar electrification of identified PVTG households.
  • PM JANMAN: Solar lighting for 1,500 Multi-Purpose Centres.
  • DAJGUA: Solarisation of 2,000 identified public institutions.
These are related components of the revised New Solar Power Scheme. The DAJGUA public-institution component should not be confused with the original PM JANMAN household component.

15. Latest Progress and Current Status

According to a Ministry of New and Renewable Energy update published on 29 July 2026:

  • The government continued implementing the New Solar Power Scheme under PM JANMAN and DAJGUA.
  • 11,838 households had been electrified through off-grid solar systems as of 30 June 2026.
  • Central Financial Assistance of ₹57.10 crore had been released as of 30 June 2026.
  • The overall household target under the scheme is one lakh identified households.
Status Note: These progress figures are dated 30 June 2026. Check the latest MNRE updates for newer installation figures and implementation information.

16. Challenges in Implementation

  • Remote locations can make transportation of solar equipment difficult.
  • Some habitations have challenging geographical conditions.
  • Solar systems require regular maintenance.
  • Battery replacement and servicing need proper planning.
  • Technical support may be difficult to arrange in isolated areas.
  • Community awareness and coordination are important for long-term success.
