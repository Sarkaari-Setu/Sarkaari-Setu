Green Energy Corridor Scheme

Strengthening India's Power Transmission Network for a Renewable Energy Future

1. Introduction

The Green Energy Corridor (GEC) is an initiative of the Government of India designed to strengthen the country's electricity transmission infrastructure and support the integration of renewable energy into the national power grid.

India generates renewable electricity from sources such as solar, wind and hydropower. However, many renewable energy projects are located far away from major electricity consumption centres.

Green Energy Corridors help transport this electricity from renewable-energy-rich regions to areas where it is needed.

Key Idea: Generating clean electricity is important, but building a reliable network to transport that electricity is equally important.

2. Launch and Implementing Ministry

  • Scheme: Green Energy Corridor (GEC)
  • Ministry: Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE)
  • Government: Government of India
  • Implementation: State Transmission Utilities (STUs) and designated transmission agencies, depending on the project.
  • Objective: Strengthening transmission infrastructure for renewable energy integration.

The programme has been developed in phases to match the growing renewable energy capacity and electricity transmission requirements of the country.

3. Main Objectives

  • Develop strong electricity transmission networks in renewable-energy-rich regions.
  • Transport solar and wind electricity to major demand centres.
  • Improve the integration of renewable energy into the national grid.
  • Reduce transmission bottlenecks and renewable electricity curtailment.
  • Improve the stability and reliability of the electricity grid.
  • Support India's long-term clean energy and energy-security goals.
  • Encourage investment in renewable energy generation projects.

4. Why Is the Scheme Necessary?

Renewable energy resources are not distributed equally across India. States with strong solar and wind potential may produce more electricity than their local consumers require at a particular time.

Without sufficient transmission infrastructure, renewable electricity may not reach the places where it is needed. This can lead to congestion and restrictions on electricity generation.

  • Solar and wind projects are often located far from large cities.
  • Renewable electricity generation can vary with weather and time of day.
  • Older transmission networks may not have sufficient capacity.
  • Electricity demand is increasing across different regions.
Green Energy Corridors help address these challenges by strengthening the electricity transmission network.

5. Major Components

  • Transmission Lines: High-voltage lines transport electricity from renewable energy generation areas to substations and demand centres.
  • Substations: These facilities help manage electricity voltage and transfer electricity between different parts of the grid.
  • Grid Modernisation: Improvements to existing transmission infrastructure help accommodate increasing renewable electricity.
  • Grid Management: Better monitoring and control help maintain the reliability and stability of the electricity network.
  • Battery Energy Storage: Battery systems can store electricity and release it when required, helping manage variations in renewable energy generation.

6. Green Energy Corridor Phase I

Phase I focused on strengthening intra-state transmission infrastructure in eight renewable-energy-rich states.

Participating states:

  • Andhra Pradesh
  • Gujarat
  • Himachal Pradesh
  • Karnataka
  • Madhya Pradesh
  • Maharashtra
  • Rajasthan
  • Tamil Nadu

The phase involved the construction and improvement of transmission lines and substations to facilitate the evacuation of renewable electricity.

Government updates have reported substantial completion of Phase I works, with the status of individual projects varying by state.

Remember: Phase I primarily focused on strengthening intra-state transmission networks.

7. Green Energy Corridor Phase II

Phase II was introduced to further expand transmission infrastructure and support the integration of additional renewable energy capacity.

Participating states:

  • Gujarat
  • Himachal Pradesh
  • Karnataka
  • Kerala
  • Rajasthan
  • Tamil Nadu
  • Uttar Pradesh

Important project details:

  • Estimated project cost: approximately ₹12,031 crore.
  • Central Financial Assistance: approximately ₹3,970 crore.
  • Central assistance rate: 33% of the project cost.
  • Renewable energy integration objective: approximately 20 GW.

The phase includes new transmission lines and substations, along with improvements to existing electricity networks. Its projects have been progressing through different stages of tendering, construction and commissioning.

Phase II is intended to support the evacuation of approximately 20 GW of renewable energy through improved intra-state transmission infrastructure.

8. Green Energy Corridor Phase III (GEC-III)

The Union Cabinet approved Green Energy Corridor Phase III on 30 September 2026. This phase represents a major expansion of India's renewable energy transmission infrastructure.

Important details:

  • Cabinet approval: 30 September 2026.
  • Total project outlay: ₹1,86,405 crore.
  • Renewable energy evacuation target: up to 135 GW.
  • Battery Energy Storage System target: 50 GWh.
  • Target completion: Financial Year 2032–33.
  • Central Financial Assistance: ₹54,082 crore.

The scheme has two major financial components:

  • Intra-State Transmission Systems: ₹1,36,378 crore for strengthening electricity transmission infrastructure.
  • Battery Energy Storage Systems: ₹50,000 crore for deploying 50 GWh of battery storage.

Battery storage will help manage renewable energy fluctuations, transmission congestion and electricity demand during non-solar hours.

Major Update: GEC-III is designed to facilitate the evacuation of up to 135 GW of renewable energy and add 50 GWh of battery storage capacity.

9. Green Energy Corridor for Ladakh

A separate inter-state transmission project was approved in October 2023 to evacuate renewable electricity from the proposed 13 GW renewable energy project in Ladakh.

Important details:

  • Renewable energy generation planned: 13 GW.
  • Associated battery storage planned: 12 GWh.
  • Estimated project cost: ₹20,773.70 crore.
  • Central Financial Assistance: ₹8,309.48 crore.
  • Assistance rate: 40% of project cost.
  • Implementing agency: Power Grid Corporation of India Limited (POWERGRID).
  • Target timeline: Financial Year 2029–30.

The project includes a high-voltage transmission system connecting Ladakh with the national electricity grid. The transmission route is planned through Himachal Pradesh and Punjab towards Kaithal in Haryana.

This is an inter-state transmission project associated with the wider Green Energy Corridor initiative.

10. Financial Assistance and Funding

The Green Energy Corridor programme uses Central Financial Assistance to support eligible transmission infrastructure projects.

Important funding information:

  • Phase II intra-state projects have an estimated cost of approximately ₹12,031 crore.
  • Phase II includes Central Financial Assistance of approximately ₹3,970 crore.
  • GEC-III has a total project outlay of ₹1,86,405 crore.
  • GEC-III includes ₹54,082 crore in Central Financial Assistance.
  • The Ladakh inter-state project has a separate approved project cost and Central Financial Assistance.
Important: These amounts are project-level funding provisions. They are not direct cash benefits or subsidies available to individual households.

11. Benefits of the Scheme

  • Better Renewable Energy Integration: Makes it easier to transport electricity generated from solar and wind projects.
  • Improved Grid Reliability: Strengthens the electricity transmission network.
  • Reduced Electricity Wastage: Helps reduce renewable electricity curtailment caused by transmission constraints.
  • Energy Security: Supports the use of domestic renewable energy resources.
  • Environmental Benefits: Helps increase the use of clean electricity and reduce dependence on fossil fuels.
  • Economic Development: Supports investment, infrastructure development and employment in the energy sector.
  • Battery Storage: Helps balance electricity supply and demand when solar and wind generation changes.

12. Who Benefits from the Scheme?

The scheme primarily supports electricity transmission infrastructure and renewable energy integration.

  • State Transmission Utilities.
  • Renewable energy developers and power generators.
  • Electricity distribution utilities.
  • Industries and commercial electricity consumers.
  • Households that depend on reliable electricity supply.
  • Regions with high solar and wind energy potential.
Note: Ordinary citizens do not apply individually for Green Energy Corridor funding. The programme supports approved transmission and storage infrastructure projects.

13. Application Process

Green Energy Corridor is an infrastructure development programme rather than an individual beneficiary scheme. It does not have a general public application form for households.

Projects are identified and developed through government planning, state transmission agencies and the relevant implementing authorities.

  • Transmission requirements are assessed.
  • Eligible infrastructure projects are planned.
  • Project proposals undergo the required approvals.
  • Funding and implementation arrangements are finalised.
  • Transmission infrastructure is constructed and commissioned.

14. Important Terms to Know

  • GW (Gigawatt): A unit of power equal to one billion watts.
  • GWh (Gigawatt-hour): A unit of energy commonly used to describe battery storage capacity.
  • Transmission Line: Infrastructure used to transport electricity over long distances.
  • Substation: A facility used to control voltage and connect different parts of the electricity network.
  • Intra-State Transmission: Electricity transmission within a state.
  • Inter-State Transmission: Electricity transmission between different states.
  • BESS: Battery Energy Storage System.
  • Renewable Energy Curtailment: A reduction in renewable electricity generation when the grid cannot accept or transport all available electricity.
