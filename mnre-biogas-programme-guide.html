MNRE Biogas Programme

Complete Guide to Biogas Plants, Financial Assistance and Application

1. What is the MNRE Biogas Programme?

The Biogas Programme is an initiative of the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE), Government of India. It promotes the installation of small and medium-sized biogas plants, particularly in rural areas.

The programme encourages households, farmers and other eligible beneficiaries to convert organic waste such as cattle dung and biodegradable waste into useful energy.

  • Promotes clean cooking fuel.
  • Supports decentralised renewable energy generation.
  • Encourages the productive use of cattle dung and organic waste.
  • Helps produce nutrient-rich organic manure.
  • Supports sanitation, rural employment and environmental protection.
The programme is implemented by MNRE through the National Bioenergy Programme. Its Phase-I included a dedicated allocation of ₹100 crore for the Biogas Programme.

2. What is Biogas?

Biogas is a renewable fuel produced when organic materials decompose in an oxygen-free environment through a process called anaerobic digestion.

Common materials used for producing biogas include:

  • Cattle dung and animal waste.
  • Agricultural residues suitable for digestion.
  • Kitchen and food waste.
  • Other biodegradable organic materials.

Biogas generally contains methane and carbon dioxide, along with small quantities of other gases. Its methane content makes it useful as a fuel.

Biogas can be used for:

  • Cooking and heating.
  • Lighting through suitable equipment.
  • Small-scale electricity generation.
  • Other eligible thermal energy applications.
The leftover digested slurry can be used as organic manure, subject to appropriate handling and use.

3. Objectives of the Programme

  • Clean Energy: Promote biogas as an alternative to conventional cooking and energy sources.
  • Waste Management: Encourage the productive use of cattle dung and biodegradable waste.
  • Organic Manure: Promote the use of digested slurry to supplement chemical fertilisers.
  • Rural Development: Support farmers, rural households and local employment.
  • Environmental Protection: Help reduce unmanaged organic waste and greenhouse gas emissions.
  • Energy Independence: Encourage decentralised energy production for local needs.

4. Types of Biogas Plants Supported

A. Small Biogas Plants

  • Capacity: 1 to 25 cubic metres of biogas per day.
  • Primarily intended for household, farm and small-scale requirements.
  • Can provide cooking fuel and other eligible small energy applications.
  • Individual beneficiaries can register their interest through the MNRE Biogas Portal.

B. Medium-Sized Biogas Plants

  • Capacity: Above 25 to 2,500 cubic metres of biogas per day.
  • Designed for larger community, institutional, dairy or commercial requirements.
  • Can support electricity generation or thermal and cooling applications.
  • Proposals are submitted through designated State Programme Implementing Agencies.
The programme's medium-plant category supports power-generation capacities in the range of 3 kW to 250 kW, or eligible thermal applications.

5. Eligibility Criteria

For Small Biogas Plants:

  • The beneficiary should have sufficient land or space for installation, generally around 50–60 square metres.
  • A regular supply of cattle dung or other suitable feedstock should be available.
  • A regular water supply should be available.
  • The beneficiary should be capable of arranging their contribution towards the project cost.
  • The proposed plant must meet the applicable technical and programme requirements.

For Medium-Sized Plants:

  • The project must meet the prescribed technical and financial requirements.
  • Sufficient organic feedstock should be available on a sustainable basis.
  • The project must be submitted through the designated implementing agency.
  • Detailed project reports may be required depending on the proposed capacity.
  • Installation, commissioning and performance verification must follow MNRE guidelines.
Meeting the basic eligibility conditions does not automatically guarantee financial assistance. Approval, available targets, budget and applicable programme guidelines also matter.

6. Financial Assistance for Small Biogas Plants

MNRE provides Central Financial Assistance (CFA) based on the capacity of the biogas plant and the beneficiary's eligible category.

Indicative CFA under Phase-I:

  • 1 m³ plant: ₹9,800 for general categories; ₹17,000 for eligible special categories.
  • 2–4 m³ plant: ₹14,350 for general categories; ₹22,000 for eligible special categories.
  • 6 m³ plant: ₹22,750 for general categories; ₹29,250 for eligible special categories.
  • 8–10 m³ plant: ₹23,000 for general categories; ₹34,500 for eligible special categories.
  • 15 m³ plant: ₹37,950 for general categories; ₹63,250 for eligible special categories.
  • 20–25 m³ plant: ₹52,800 for general categories; ₹70,400 for eligible special categories.

The higher assistance categories include specified North-Eastern and hilly States, Island regions, and eligible Scheduled Caste and Scheduled Tribe beneficiaries, as defined in the guidelines.

An additional fixed assistance of ₹1,600 may be available for eligible biogas plants linked with sanitary toilets or approved biogas slurry filter units, subject to the programme conditions and applicable plant-size limits.
These are Phase-I assistance figures, not a guarantee of a currently open subsidy. The Phase-I programme period was extended only up to 30 September 2026 in the latest official parliamentary information located. Check for a fresh government notification before making financial commitments.

7. Financial Assistance for Medium Biogas Plants

For medium-sized plants, the assistance is calculated according to eligible power-generation capacity or thermal application.

  • 3 kW to 50 kW: Up to ₹45,000 per kW for power generation, or ₹22,500 per kW equivalent for thermal and cooling applications.
  • Above 50 kW to 200 kW: Up to ₹40,000 per kW for power generation, or ₹20,000 per kW equivalent for thermal and cooling applications.
  • Above 200 kW to 250 kW: Up to ₹35,000 per kW for power generation, or ₹17,500 per kW equivalent for thermal and cooling applications.

Eligible projects in specified North-Eastern and Island regions, registered Gaushalas, and SC/ST beneficiary categories may receive an additional 20% over the standard CFA, subject to the scheme guidelines.

The assistance is subject to prescribed ceilings, project approval, performance requirements and available funds. It should not be treated as a fixed payment for every project.

8. Additional Benefits and Incentives

  • Biogas Generator Incentive: An additional ₹10,000 incentive was provided for eligible biogas-based generator sets or biogas-engine water-pumping systems connected with plants of 10–25 m³ capacity.
  • Turnkey Job Fee: Under the specified arrangements, ₹3,000 for plants of 1–10 m³ and ₹5,000 for plants of 15–25 m³ could be provided for eligible construction, supervision and commissioning work.
  • Technical Support: Biogas Development and Training Centres provide technical assistance and training.
  • Maintenance Support: Eligible turnkey arrangements include a prescribed five-year trouble-free operation and maintenance warranty.
Additional incentives are subject to the plant design, technical conditions and applicable guidelines. They are not automatically available for every installation.

9. Application Process

For Small Biogas Plants:

  • Visit the official MNRE Biogas Portal.
  • Register your interest in installing a biogas plant.
  • Provide the required personal, location and project details.
  • Submit the required information and documents.
  • The designated implementing agency processes applications according to allocated targets and programme conditions.
  • After approval and installation, the plant is inspected and commissioned.

For Medium-Sized Biogas Plants:

  • Contact the designated State Programme Implementing Agency.
  • Prepare the project proposal and required technical documents.
  • Submit the proposal through the MNRE Biogas Portal via the implementing agency.
  • Obtain the required administrative approval.
  • Install and commission the plant according to the approved specifications.
  • Complete the prescribed performance verification and documentation.
Visit MNRE Biogas Portal
Portal registration or submission of interest does not mean that an application has been approved or that subsidy funds are guaranteed.

10. How is the Subsidy Paid?

Under the Phase-I guidelines, financial assistance for eligible small biogas plants is released on a reimbursement basis after successful construction and commissioning.

  • The plant must be installed according to the prescribed specifications.
  • Required accessories and pipelines must be installed.
  • The plant must successfully generate biogas for use.
  • The completion and commissioning certificate must be issued.
  • After verification, eligible assistance is released through the prescribed Direct Benefit Transfer process.

If the beneficiary has taken an eligible bank loan, the assistance may be credited to the biogas loan account as prescribed by the guidelines.

The beneficiary may need to arrange the remaining project cost. The subsidy should not be assumed to be an advance payment.

11. Types of Biogas Plant Models

  • Deenbandhu Fixed Dome Model: A fixed-dome design commonly used for small biogas installations.
  • KVIC Floating Dome Model: Uses a movable gas holder to collect the produced biogas.
  • Prefabricated RCC Models: Use prefabricated reinforced cement concrete components.
  • Flexi-Bag Plants: Use flexible materials as the digester and gas-storage structure.

The appropriate model depends on the location, available feedstock, installation space, expected gas requirement and technical suitability.

12. Documents and Information Required

The exact documents depend on the beneficiary category, plant capacity and implementing agency.

  • Applicant's identification and contact details.
  • Address and proposed plant installation location.
  • Land or site availability information.
  • Details of cattle, dung or other available feedstock.
  • Bank account details for eligible financial assistance.
  • Category certificate, where applicable for special assistance.
  • Technical project documents for medium-sized plants.
  • Other documents requested by the implementing agency.
Submit documents only through official government channels or the authorised implementing agency. Never share banking passwords, OTPs or PINs.

13. Benefits of the Biogas Programme

  • Provides an alternative source of cooking fuel.
  • Helps households and farms make productive use of organic waste.
  • Produces slurry that can be used as organic manure.
  • Can reduce dependence on conventional fuels.
  • Encourages decentralised renewable energy production.
  • Supports rural livelihood and technical employment opportunities.
  • Contributes to better organic waste management.

14. Latest Scheme Status – November 2026

The MNRE Biogas Programme was implemented as part of Phase-I of the National Bioenergy Programme for FY 2021–22 to FY 2025–26.

  • Phase-I originally had an approved overall National Bioenergy Programme outlay of ₹858 crore.
  • ₹100 crore was allocated for the Biogas Programme within Phase-I.
  • In a parliamentary reply dated 22 July 2026, the government stated that Phase-I had been extended up to 30 September 2026.
  • The same reply stated that Phase-II of the National Bioenergy Programme had not been approved at that time.
As of November 2026, do not assume that the earlier Phase-I subsidy rates or application arrangements are currently open. Check MNRE announcements for any further extension, revised guidelines or new phase.

15. Difference Between Biogas and Compressed Biogas (CBG)

Biogas:

  • Produced by anaerobic digestion of organic materials.
  • Generally used locally for cooking, heating and eligible power applications.
  • Contains methane along with carbon dioxide and other gases.

Compressed Biogas (CBG):

  • Produced by upgrading raw biogas to remove carbon dioxide and other impurities.
  • Has a higher methane concentration.
  • Can be compressed for suitable transport and fuel applications.
  • Generally requires larger processing, purification and compression infrastructure.
The MNRE Biogas Programme and the government's GOBARdhan National Circular Bioenergy Scheme are different initiatives. They have different objectives, beneficiaries and support structures.
